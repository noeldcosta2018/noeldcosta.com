SAP Business One costs about $3,200 per Professional user or $1,700 per Limited user one-time, or roughly $110 to $219 per user per month in the cloud. Implementation adds $25,000 to $150,000 or more, and on-premise maintenance adds 18 to 20% of licence value every year.

Short answer: for a 10-user company, plan on about $21,500 in perpetual licences plus $25,000 to $50,000 to implement. Partners set the real price for your country, so treat these as published benchmarks, checked October 2026.

Most clients who reach out to me ask the same four questions. What does the licence cost? What does implementation add?

What keeps costing after go-live? How do I bring the total down? In 2026 there is a fifth: when do I stay on Business One, and when do I move to GROW with SAP?

## What SAP Business One is, and why there is no price list

[SAP Business One](https://www.sap.com/products/erp/business-one.html) is SAP's ERP for small and midsize companies. It puts finance, sales, purchasing, inventory and reporting in one system. It runs on SAP HANA or Microsoft SQL Server, with the same application on both.

SAP does not sell Business One directly to most customers, and it does not publish a public price list. Certified partners sell it, and the partner sets the local price, the discount and the currency. That is why two quotes for the same ten users can differ.

The figures below come from published partner price lists and pricing benchmarks, checked in October 2026. Use them to test a quote, not to replace one.

Business One is not SAP S/4HANA. Above it sits GROW with SAP, built on SAP S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition, and RISE with SAP for larger companies.

## SAP Business One licence types and prices

There are three licence types most companies need: Professional, Limited and the Starter Package. Pick by what each person does in the system, not by job title.

The table shows published benchmark prices in US dollars, checked October 2026. Perpetual prices are one-time per user and exclude maintenance. Cloud prices are per user per month.

| Licence type | What it covers | Perpetual, one-time | Cloud, per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Professional | Full access: finance, sales, purchasing, inventory, administration | About $3,200 | About $188 to $219 |
| Limited: Financial, CRM or Logistics | One area only: accounting and banking, or customers and sales, or purchasing and inventory | About $1,700 | About $112 to $158 |
| Starter Package | Core functions for small teams, maximum of five users | About $1,400 | About $110 to $149 |

Sources: [CostBench SAP Business One pricing](https://www.costbench.com/software/erp/sap-business-one/) (verified August 2026; cloud range is multi-tenant to single-tenant hosting). An Australian SAP partner, [Cloud Factory](https://www.cloudfactory.co/solutions/erp/sap-business-one/pricing), publishes a higher list: $3,786 Professional and $1,963 Financial or CRM user, one-time, with currency not stated on the page.

Partners and regions vary. One benchmark site, ERP Research, puts a perpetual Professional user at $3,500 to $5,500, so use the table as a floor for questions, not a promise.

### Professional or Limited: who needs which

The most common mistake I see is buying Professional licences for everyone. Most people in a small business work in one area. A Limited licence covers them at roughly half the price.

A European distribution client in 2023 over-provisioned Professional licences. The fix is simple to describe and tedious to do: list every user, list what they touch, and license to that list.

The arithmetic makes the point. A 10-person company with 3 Professional and 7 Limited users pays about $21,500 in perpetual licences. Give all ten people Professional licences and the bill becomes $32,000, with maintenance rising in step.

### The Starter Package

The Starter Package is for up to five users with simple processes. Availability varies by country, so ask your partner whether it is offered locally and what it leaves out.

A GCC retail client in 2024 used the Starter Package to prove value before scaling. That is the right use of it: a low first commitment, then Professional and Limited licences once the business knows what it needs.

### Indirect access

Indirect access covers people or external systems that reach Business One data through an integration rather than logging in. SAP has sold it as separate price list items, per user and per instance, according to [ASUG's summary of SAP's Business One licensing changes](https://asug.com/insights/b1-sap-business-one-pricing-and-licensing-changes-you-need-to-know).

I could not find a current public price for these items, so I will not give one. If you plan a webstore, a warehouse app or a portal that reads or writes Business One data, ask your partner in writing which indirect access licences apply.

## Cloud or on-premise: which costs less

This choice changes the shape of the cost more than its size.

**On-premise.** Perpetual licences paid up front, plus annual maintenance at 18 to 20% of licence value. You provide the server and the database licence, and you plan and apply upgrades.

**Cloud.** A monthly fee per user, hosted by SAP or a partner. Upgrades are included, and year one costs less, but you pay for as long as you use it.

Take the 10-user example. In the cloud at the lower benchmark rates (3 Professional at $188, 7 Limited at $112), that is about $1,350 a month, or roughly $81,000 over five years.

On-premise is $21,500 plus five years of maintenance, about $41,000 to $43,000 in total. That is before the server, the database licence and the IT time to run them. Run both models over five years with your own partner's numbers before you decide.

## SAP Business One implementation cost

Licences are the starting point. Implementation is usually the bigger number, and the one that moves most.

Expect $25,000 to $150,000 or more, and 90 to 150 days for most deployments. That is a market range.

It sits inside the small-business total I use across the 21 SAP programmes I have led or rescued: $80,000 to $500,000 once licences, hosting, training and support are added. The [SAP implementation cost guide](/sap-implementation-cost-and-budget-breakdown/) explains how that total builds up.

This table breaks the implementation range down by size.

| Business size | Typical implementation range | What drives it |
|---|---|---|
| Small (5 to 15 users) | $25,000 to $50,000 | Core modules, light data migration, standard configuration |
| Mid-sized (15 to 50 users) | $50,000 to $100,000 | More modules, integrations, heavier data migration |
| Larger or complex | $100,000 to $150,000+ | Custom workflows, legacy integration, extensive data cleanup |

Four things push the number up:

1. **Users and modules.** More of either means more configuration, testing and training.
2. **Data quality.** Clean records migrate quickly. Customer and item data scattered across spreadsheets does not.
3. **Integrations.** A webstore, a payment gateway, a logistics provider. Each one is scope, and some need indirect access licences.
4. **Customisation.** Bending standard processes to match old habits adds build time and makes every future upgrade harder.

Simple processes and fast decisions shorten the timeline. Messy data and slow sign-offs stretch it.

## Ongoing costs after go-live

Go-live is not the end of the spend.

**Maintenance (on-premise).** 18 to 20% of licence value per year. On $21,500 of licences, that is about $3,900 to $4,300 annually.

**Hosting.** Your own server and database licence on-premise, or the hosting share built into the cloud fee. Single-tenant cloud hosting costs more per user than multi-tenant.

**Upgrades (on-premise).** Maintenance gives you the right to new releases. Someone still has to plan, test and apply each one, which means consultant days if you customised heavily.

**Support.** Partner support after hypercare is usually a separate contract, priced as a retainer or a block of hours. Ask for it in the first quote, not after go-live.

**Training.** Key-user training from a partner is billed at consultant day rates, about $1,000 to $2,000 a day. It recurs, because people leave and processes change.

Fill in each line before the first partner meeting. If a partner's quote leaves a line blank, ask why.

| Cost line | How to estimate it | Benchmark (checked October 2026) |
|---|---|---|

| Limited licences | Everyone else, by area: Financial, CRM or Logistics | About $1,700 each, or $112 to $158 a month |
| Indirect access | List every system that reads or writes Business One data | Partner quote |
| Database and hosting | HANA or SQL Server; your server or the partner's cloud | Partner quote |
| Implementation | Size band from the table above | $25,000 to $150,000+ |
| Data migration | Count customer, supplier and item records; note where they live | Often inside implementation; confirm |
| Training | Key users at go-live, plus refreshers each year | $1,000 to $2,000 per consultant day |
| Support after go-live | Retainer or block of hours | Partner quote |
| Annual maintenance (on-premise) | 18 to 20% of total licence value | e.g. $3,900 to $4,300 on $21,500 |

## How to bring the total down

1. **Map your processes before you ask for a quote.** Write down how an order becomes an invoice and how month-end closes today. Without it, you pay for discovery twice.
2. **Start small.** With five users or fewer and simple processes, ask about the Starter Package. Upgrade when the business needs it.
3. **Match licences to roles.** Professional for the few who cross areas. Limited for everyone else.
4. **Stay close to standard.** Every customisation is paid for twice: once to build, again at every upgrade.
5. **Get two or three partner quotes.** Prices are set locally, and the spread between partners is real.

A partner who recommends maximum scope before asking about your processes is selling services, not solving your problem. Start with what you need, prove the value, then add modules.

## Regional notes: Malaysia, the Philippines and the GCC

SAP does not publish Business One prices in ringgit, pesos or dirhams. Local partners set them, so the US dollar benchmarks above are a check, not a quote. What differs by country is often compliance scope, and that changes implementation cost.

**Malaysia.** LHDN has phased in mandatory e-invoicing, with the last phase (revenue up to RM1 million) from 1 July 2026 and businesses under RM500,000 exempt ([NST, June 2025](https://www.nst.com.my/amp/news/nation/2025/06/1226501/lhdn-e-invoice-implementation-revenue-below-rm5mil-deferred-jan-1-2026)). Ask your partner how MyInvois submission is handled and whether it is priced as an add-on.

**The Philippines.** Computerised accounting systems must be registered with the BIR, and taxpayers using them face e-invoicing obligations under the EIS ([Appcentric, July 2026](https://www.appcentric.ph/blog/sap-cas-accreditation-bir-eis-philippines)). Ask for the partner's BIR registration track record and put the compliance work in the quote.

**The GCC.** The GCC retail client above shows the route I would take for a first deployment: start with the Starter Package, prove value, then scale. VAT and e-invoicing scope varies by country, so confirm it per entity.

## When to move from Business One to GROW with SAP

Stay on Business One when your processes are standard, you run one or a few companies, and your user count is in the tens rather than the hundreds. For that profile it is the cheaper, faster route.

Look at GROW with SAP when the business is heading somewhere Business One was not built for:

1. You are adding subsidiaries in several countries and need group reporting without spreadsheets.
2. Your system depends on a growing pile of partner add-ons to do core work.
3. You need enterprise functions, such as deeper manufacturing or project accounting.
4. You expect your user count to multiply within five years.

GROW programmes often take about 6 to 9 months, against 90 to 150 days for Business One. If you are at one signal, Business One is probably still right; at three, start planning. My [GROW with SAP guide](/sap-implementation/grow-with-sap/) covers the other side, and the [small business ERP guide](/best-erp-for-small-business-operations/) compares the wider field.

### Whether you need an ERP yet

For a business running on disconnected spreadsheets, the consolidation usually pays for it. Fewer inventory errors, a faster month-end close, and orders that reach purchasing without being re-keyed.

For a small business whose operations already run well, the payback is slower. If month-end is not a bottleneck and you do not lose orders between systems, you may not need a full ERP yet.
