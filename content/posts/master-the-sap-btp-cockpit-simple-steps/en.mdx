The SAP Business Technology Platform Cockpit is the central admin interface for managing your SAP cloud environment. Applications, services, security settings, subscriptions: all of it runs through here.

Three main groups use the Cockpit. Technical admins handle configuration and monitoring. Developers deploy and manage applications. Business users access specific tools, each role uses different sections. Most companies buy SAP cloud products and do not get full value because nobody took the time to learn the Cockpit properly. Tasks that should take minutes take hours. Costs accumulate from unused services. Security gaps appear in entitlements nobody audited.

Access the Cockpit at `https://account.hana.ondemand.com`. For trial accounts: `https://account.hanatrial.ondemand.com`. You need an SAP user ID: ask your administrator if you do not have one.

## What SAP BTP solves

BTP addresses three categories of problem that come up in every SAP cloud engagement.

**Integration.** SAP Cloud Integration (formerly CPI) connects S/4HANA, SuccessFactors, Ariba, and non-SAP systems like Salesforce using visual integration flows. API Management exposes business functions to other applications. Event Mesh handles system-to-system communication based on business events. Open Connectors provides pre-built integrations for hundreds of third-party systems.

**Custom development without touching the core.** ABAP Cloud Environment lets developers build extensions without modifying core SAP code (which keeps upgrade paths clear). Cloud Foundry runtime handles Java, Node.js, and Python. Business Application Studio gives developers a browser-based IDE. SAP Build gives business users low-code tools for simple applications.

**Data management.** HANA Cloud provides in-memory database capabilities. Datasphere (formerly Data Warehouse Cloud) combines data from multiple sources for business reporting. Data Intelligence Cloud creates pipelines for moving and transforming data between systems.

## Setting up BTP for daily use

The next six sections cover the components of BTP administration that you will use most often: account structure, identity, services, deployment, integration, and monitoring. Each one has a setup decision you make once and live with for the life of the platform.

### Subaccounts and entitlements

This is where most teams create a mess that haunts them for years.

Create separate subaccounts for development, testing, and production. In your global account, click "Create Subaccount" and use a consistent naming convention: something like "Finance-Dev", "Finance-Test", "Finance-Prod". I worked with a client who had 50+ randomly named subaccounts and nobody could find anything. Rebuilding that structure mid-project is painful.

For multi-environment setups: mirror your subaccount structure across regions if you are a global business. Keep development entitlements minimal. Give production environments priority for performance-sensitive resources, and use labels to group related subaccounts.

**Entitlements** control which services each subaccount can access and how much of each from your global account, go to "Entitlements" → "Configure Entitlements", select the subaccount, and add services. Your global account has a finite pool of resources to distribute: start minimal and add as needed. Many services have a free tier, which is useful for testing.

Always document your entitlement distribution somewhere outside the Cockpit. The platform does not track your decisions or the reasoning behind them.

### User management and authentication

Go to "Security" → "Users" in your subaccount. Add users individually or upload a CSV for bulk additions.

If your organization has an identity provider (Azure AD, Okta, or similar), connect it rather than managing users manually. Go to "Security" → "Trust Configuration" and exchange metadata files between SAP and your IdP. This eliminates most authentication headaches and centralizes user lifecycle management.

For enterprise setups, custom SAML identity providers give you the most flexibility. The default SAP ID Service works for testing and small deployments.

Role collections are bundles of permissions assigned to users. My approach:
1. Create role collections based on job functions, not individuals
2. Keep them specific enough to be meaningful but not so granular you have hundreds to maintain
3. Include a break-glass "admin" role collection for emergencies
4. Review role assignments quarterly: people change jobs but often keep old permissions

Enable two-factor authentication, restrict access to trusted networks where possible, and check the "Security Alerts" section regularly. SAP uses it to flag potential issues in your environment.

### Service instances and applications

Creating a service instance:
1. Go to "Services" → "Service Marketplace" in your subaccount
2. Find the service you need and click "Create"
3. Select your plan: this controls capability and cost
4. Configure the required settings and save them externally (you will need them when troubleshooting)

After creating a service, bind it to your application under "Service Bindings". BTP handles the connection and credentials.

**Service keys** are credentials for external tools or teams that need to access the service without being BTP users. Name them descriptively: `dev-team-access` or `jenkins-deployment`, not `key1`.

In the Service Marketplace: use the search bar when you know what you need, and use "Recently Used" for repeat access. Filter by environment (Cloud Foundry, Kyma) when exploring, and start with free plans for anything you are testing.

### Deploying applications

The BTP Cockpit supports three runtime environments. Choose based on your team's skills and your application's requirements.

| Runtime | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud Foundry | Java, Node.js, Python applications; traditional web apps | Most mature environment; large service catalog |
| Kyma | Kubernetes-native workloads; microservices with complex scaling | More flexible but requires Kubernetes knowledge |
| ABAP Cloud | ABAP-based extensions; clean core development | Familiar to SAP developers; integrates directly with S/4HANA |

I have seen projects delayed by months because developers were pushed into an environment that matched the architecture plan but not their skills. Match the runtime to the team.

Deploying to Cloud Foundry:
1. Create a manifest.yml file specifying memory, instances, build packs, and environment variables
2. Push using the CF CLI or automate through the BTP Cockpit
3. BTP detects the build pack automatically
4. Environment variables are injected, services are bound, and routes are configured

Automate repetitive deployments with BTP CLI and APIs as early as possible. Manual deployment processes are a time sink at scale. Automated deployments cut setup time by 80% on one engagement: the investment pays back quickly.

### Integration and extension scenarios

| Scenario | What it enables | How to set it up |
|---|---|---|
| S/4HANA integration | Share data between S/4HANA and BTP applications | Set up Cloud Connector → configure Destination Service → access S/4HANA OData APIs |
| SuccessFactors extension | Build custom HR workflows using SuccessFactors data | Enable SAP Event Mesh + API Management → extend with CAP or Node.js |
| Third-party integration | Connect Salesforce, Workday, or any non-SAP system | Use Open Connectors pre-built connectors or build custom via Integration Suite |
| On-premise connectivity | Secure access to internal systems from BTP | Install Cloud Connector on your network → configure in Cockpit under "Connectivity" |
| API exposure | Expose BTP services or S/4HANA functions as APIs | Use API Management → design and publish API products → monitor consumption |

The Cloud Connector is the bridge between your on-premise network and BTP. It creates a secure tunnel without requiring inbound firewall rules. Configure it to expose only the specific systems and paths you need: minimum access principle applies here too.

### Monitoring and analytics

Set up monitoring before you need it, not after something breaks. In your subaccount, go to "Monitoring" and configure alerts for the services that matter. My standard setup: email alerts for critical applications, webhook notifications for services that cannot go down.

What to watch:
1. **Application health**: response times and error rates per application
2. **Service status**: availability and quota consumption for key services
3. **Usage analytics**: resource consumption by service, subaccount, and time period: useful for catching waste before it becomes a cost problem

Delete unused services monthly. Scale down development environments outside business hours. Check entitlement consumption before renewing contracts: unused allocations are a common source of overspend.

The Cockpit shows current state. It does not show you trends unless you configure dashboards. Configure usage dashboards for your highest-cost services from day one.

## Advanced features

**Custom domains** improve user adoption. Configure them under "Security" → "Custom Domain" for each subaccount. The professional appearance makes a genuine difference to end users who would otherwise see technical SAP URLs.

**Automation via BTP CLI and APIs** removes manual steps from repeated processes. Common automations: environment provisioning, deployment pipelines, entitlement distribution across new subaccounts. The CLI connects to the Cockpit via API and lets you script everything the UI does.

**Extension Factory** (in the Kyma environment) provides a runtime for extending SAP systems with event-driven microservices. Useful for capabilities that need to react to SAP events without modifying core systems.

**ABAP Cloud** environments in BTP provide a managed system for running ABAP-based extensions while keeping S/4HANA core clean. Extensions built here follow the clean core principle: they interact with S/4HANA through stable APIs rather than direct table access.

## What to avoid

1. Creating subaccounts without a naming convention before you create any
2. Assigning roles to individuals instead of role collections: the administration overhead compounds over time
3. Ignoring the "Security Alerts" section
4. Letting development services run in production configurations
5. Not documenting your account structure and the reasoning behind it: the Cockpit stores the what, not the why

<summary>What is the SAP BTP Cockpit and what does it do?</summary>

The SAP BTP Cockpit is the web-based administration interface for managing your SAP Business Technology Platform environment. It is where you create and manage subaccounts, configure entitlements, manage users and security, deploy applications, subscribe to services, and monitor your cloud environment.

Access it at `https://account.hana.ondemand.com` (or `https://account.hanatrial.ondemand.com` for trial accounts). You need an SAP user ID: if you do not have one, your company's SAP administrator sets it up.

<details>
<summary>How should I structure subaccounts in SAP BTP?</summary>

Separate development, testing, and production into distinct subaccounts from the start. Name them consistently (something like "Finance-Dev", "Finance-Test", "Finance-Prod") so the environment and function are clear from the name alone.

For larger organizations, consider separating by department or business unit as well. The structure you create early is difficult to change later without disrupting running services. Before creating anything, spend time mapping out the structure on paper. I have seen teams spend weeks reorganizing because they started without a plan: clients with 50+ randomly named subaccounts where nobody could locate services quickly.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the four pillars of SAP BTP?</summary>

Database and Data Management. HANA Cloud, Datasphere, and data pipeline tools for storage, processing, and analytics. Application Development. Cloud Foundry, Kyma, ABAP Cloud, and Business Application Studio for building extensions and applications. Integration. SAP Cloud Integration, API Management, Event Mesh, and Open Connectors for connecting systems and processes. Intelligent Technologies. AI, machine learning, and analytics capabilities including SAP Joule and AI Core.

These four pillars define what you can build and connect through the Cockpit. Most BTP projects touch integration and application development first, then expand into data and intelligence as the landscape matures.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do user management and role collections work in SAP BTP?</summary>

Users are managed per subaccount under "Security" → "Users". For enterprise setups, connect your corporate identity provider (Azure AD, Okta, or similar) via SAML trust configuration instead of managing users manually in BTP. This centralizes user lifecycle management and removes a class of administrative overhead.

Role collections are bundles of permissions assigned to users. Create them based on job functions, not individuals. Common examples: "Developer-Subaccount-Admin", "Business-User-Read-Only", "Integration-Administrator". Review assignments quarterly: people change roles but permissions often persist. An emergency "admin" role collection with full access is worth maintaining for break-glass scenarios.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Which runtime environment should I choose in BTP. Cloud Foundry, Kyma, or ABAP Cloud?</summary>

Match the environment to your team's existing skills, not just the architecture diagram. Cloud Foundry is the most mature and has the largest service catalog: use it for Java, Node.js, and Python applications. Kyma is Kubernetes-native and flexible, but requires Kubernetes knowledge that many SAP-focused teams do not have. ABAP Cloud is for ABAP-based extensions and is the natural choice for teams with SAP development backgrounds.

I have seen projects delayed by months because the runtime was chosen based on architectural principles without checking what the developers actually knew. The technically superior choice that the team cannot implement effectively is worse than the pragmatic choice they can.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I connect SAP BTP to on-premise SAP systems?</summary>

Install the Cloud Connector on your on-premise network. It creates a secure outbound tunnel to BTP without requiring inbound firewall changes. Configure it to expose only the specific systems and URL paths that BTP needs: minimum access principle.

In the BTP Cockpit, go to your subaccount's "Connectivity" section to verify the Cloud Connector connection and check its status. Then create Destinations pointing to your exposed SAP systems. Your BTP applications and integration flows reference these Destinations when they need to call on-premise systems. Test the full path from BTP to your on-premise system before moving anything to production.

</details>
