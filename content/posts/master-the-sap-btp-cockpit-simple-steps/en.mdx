The SAP Business Technology Platform (BTP) Cockpit is the web console where you run your SAP cloud environment: accounts, services, users, security, deployed applications and cost. If you administer BTP, deploy to it or approve its bill, this is the screen you will live in.

To get in, open the regional gateway for your region: `https://emea.cockpit.btp.cloud.sap`, `https://amer.cockpit.btp.cloud.sap` or `https://apac.cockpit.btp.cloud.sap` ([SAP Learning](https://learning.sap.com/courses/operating-sap-business-technology-platform/utilizing-sap-btp-cockpit)). You need an SAP user ID; your administrator can set one up. Trial accounts use `https://cockpit.hanatrial.ondemand.com/trial/` and last up to 90 days, with an extension needed after 30 ([SAP Developers](https://developers.sap.com/tutorials/cp-trial-quick-onboarding.html)).

Then set things up in the order below. I spent days working this out the first time. Most of the pain came from doing step four before step one.

## How the Cockpit is organised

Everything sits in a hierarchy. The **global account** is your contract with SAP and holds the pool of services you bought. **Directories** group subaccounts, for example by business unit. **Subaccounts** are where the work happens: each one has a region, an environment such as Cloud Foundry or Kyma, its own users and its own service instances.

BTP itself covers four areas: application development and automation, integration, data and analytics, and AI. You will meet all four in the Cockpit, but most programmes start with integration and extensions to S/4HANA.

## First-week setup sequence

This is the order I follow on a new global account. Each step has a natural owner.

1. **Agree the subaccount structure and naming** (platform owner). Development, test and production per area, for example `Finance-Dev`, `Finance-Test`, `Finance-Prod`.
2. **Connect your identity provider** (security lead). Do this before you add a single user.
3. **Define role collections by job function** (security lead), including one break-glass admin collection.
4. **Distribute entitlements, minimal first** (platform owner). Add more when a team can show it needs them.
5. **Install and configure the Cloud Connector** (infrastructure team) if BTP has to reach on-premise systems.
6. **Switch on alerting and usage review** (platform owner). Decide who looks at what, and how often.
7. **Write it all down outside the Cockpit** (architect). Structure, naming rules, entitlement split and the reasons for each.

### Subaccounts and entitlements

This is where most teams create a mess that follows them for years.

I once worked with a client who had 50+ randomly named subaccounts, and nobody could find anything. Untangling that mid-project is painful. Take a day to map the structure on paper first. If you run in several regions, mirror the structure across them and use labels to group related subaccounts.

**Entitlements** decide which services each subaccount can use, and how much. Your global account has a finite pool. I start minimal and scale up as needed, and that approach has saved my clients thousands in unused services. Many services have a free plan, which is enough for testing.

### Users, trust and role collections

If your company already has an identity provider such as Microsoft Entra ID or Okta, do not manage BTP users by hand. SAP's recommended route is a SAP Cloud Identity Services tenant, connected to the subaccount under **Security > Trust Configuration**. That tenant then acts as a proxy to your corporate identity provider. The trust can be set up automatically with OpenID Connect. Two-factor authentication and access policies then sit in one place.

Role collections are bundles of roles you assign to users or groups. My approach:

1. Build them around job functions, not individuals.
2. Keep them specific enough to mean something, but not so granular that you maintain hundreds.
3. Keep one break-glass admin collection for emergencies.
4. Review assignments every quarter. People change jobs and keep their old access.

### Services, instances and keys

To create a service instance, open **Services > Service Marketplace** in the subaccount, pick the service and choose a plan. The plan controls both capability and cost. Save the settings you chose somewhere outside the Cockpit; you will need them when something breaks.

Bind the instance to your application and BTP injects the credentials. For external tools that are not BTP applications, create a **service key**. Name keys for who uses them, such as `jenkins-deployment`, not `key1`.

## Building on BTP

### Choosing a runtime

The Cockpit offers three main environments. Choose with your team's skills in front of you.

| Runtime | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud Foundry | Java, Node.js and Python apps, including SAP Cloud Application Programming Model (CAP) | The mature default; least surprise for most teams |
| Kyma | Kubernetes-native microservices and event-driven extensions | Needs Kubernetes skills many SAP teams do not have |
| ABAP environment | ABAP Cloud extensions next to S/4HANA | Natural fit for ABAP developers; uses released APIs only |

I have seen projects delayed by months because developers had to learn a new environment that matched the architecture plan but not their experience. The technically better runtime that your team cannot build in is the worse choice.

To deploy to Cloud Foundry, describe the app in a `manifest.yml` (memory, instances, buildpack, environment variables), then push it with the CF CLI or a pipeline. Cloud Foundry detects the buildpack, binds the services and sets up routes.

### Connecting BTP to S/4HANA and other systems

Most BTP work is integration. These are the patterns I set up most often.

| Scenario | How to set it up |
|---|---|
| S/4HANA on-premise or Private Edition | Cloud Connector on your network, a destination in the subaccount, then OData or SOAP APIs |
| SAP cloud apps such as SuccessFactors | Destinations plus Integration Suite or Event Mesh, with extensions in CAP |
| Non-SAP systems such as Salesforce or Workday | Integration Suite adapters, or custom integration flows |
| Exposing your own APIs | API Management in Integration Suite: design, publish, monitor |

The Cloud Connector opens an outbound tunnel to BTP, so you do not need inbound firewall rules. Expose only the systems and URL paths BTP actually needs. If integration is your main use, my [SAP CPI article](/sap-cpi/) covers the design choices in more depth.

## Running BTP day to day

### Monitoring and cost control

Set up monitoring before you need it. I check my dashboards every morning with coffee. It has become a ritual, and it has saved me from more than a few "why is the system down?" moments.

What to set up:

1. **Alerts.** The SAP Alert Notification service sends platform and application events to email, Slack or your own alert tool. My usual setup is email for critical apps and a Slack webhook for anything that cannot go down.
2. **Application health.** Response times and error rates per application, from the Cloud Foundry or Kyma views.
3. **Usage and cost.** Usage Analytics in each subaccount, and Costs and Usage at global account level. Usage values refresh every 24 hours.

Delete unused service instances every month. Scale development spaces down outside working hours. Check entitlement consumption before you renew, because unused allocations are a common source of overspend.

### Automation and custom domains

Clicking through the Cockpit does not scale. The BTP command-line interface (btp CLI) and the platform APIs can script almost everything the UI does: creating subaccounts, assigning entitlements, provisioning developers.

On one engagement I needed environments for 12 new developers. Instead of clicking through the Cockpit all day, I ran my script and went for coffee. When I got back, everything was ready. Automating deployments cut setup time by 80 percent on one engagement, and wiring service keys and the CF CLI into a pipeline took deployment from hours to minutes.

Custom domains are worth the effort for user-facing apps. They are configured through the SAP Custom Domain service rather than a Cockpit menu. On one client project, executives noticed the more professional feel straight away.

## Mistakes I see most often

1. Creating subaccounts before agreeing a naming convention.
2. Assigning roles to individuals instead of role collections and groups.
3. Running development services on production-sized plans.
4. Leaving alerting until after the first outage.
5. Not documenting the account structure and the reasons behind it. The Cockpit stores the what, never the why.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is the SAP BTP Cockpit used for?</summary>

It is the web-based administration console for SAP Business Technology Platform. You use it to create subaccounts, distribute entitlements, manage users and trust, create service instances, deploy and monitor applications, and track usage and cost.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the SAP BTP Cockpit login URL?</summary>

Use your regional gateway: `https://emea.cockpit.btp.cloud.sap` for Europe, the Middle East and Africa, `https://amer.cockpit.btp.cloud.sap` for the Americas, or `https://apac.cockpit.btp.cloud.sap` for Asia-Pacific. Trial accounts use `https://cockpit.hanatrial.ondemand.com/trial/`. You need an SAP user ID to log in.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How should I structure subaccounts in SAP BTP?</summary>

Separate development, test and production into their own subaccounts from the start, and name them so the area and stage are obvious, such as `Finance-Dev` and `Finance-Prod`. Larger organisations add directories per business unit. Plan it on paper first; the structure is hard to change once services are running.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the four pillars of SAP BTP?</summary>

SAP groups BTP into application development and automation, integration, data and analytics, and artificial intelligence. Most programmes start with integration and S/4HANA extensions, then add data and AI use cases once the platform is stable.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I connect my identity provider to SAP BTP?</summary>

Set up a SAP Cloud Identity Services tenant and establish trust with your subaccount under Security > Trust Configuration; OpenID Connect can do this automatically. Then connect your corporate identity provider, such as Microsoft Entra ID or Okta, to that tenant. Users and two-factor authentication are then managed in one place.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Which BTP runtime should I choose: Cloud Foundry, Kyma or ABAP environment?</summary>

Match it to your team. Cloud Foundry suits Java, Node.js and Python apps and is the safest default. Kyma suits Kubernetes-native microservices but needs Kubernetes skills. The ABAP environment suits ABAP developers building clean core extensions next to S/4HANA.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I connect SAP BTP to an on-premise SAP system?</summary>

Install the Cloud Connector inside your network. It opens a secure outbound tunnel to BTP, so no inbound firewall changes are needed. Expose only the systems and paths BTP needs, check the connection under Connectivity in the subaccount, then create destinations that your apps and integration flows call. Test the whole path before production.

</details>
