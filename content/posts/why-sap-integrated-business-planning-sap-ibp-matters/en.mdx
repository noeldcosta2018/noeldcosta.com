SAP Integrated Business Planning (IBP) is SAP's cloud suite for supply chain planning: sales and operations planning (S&OP), demand, inventory, and response and supply, plus demand-driven replenishment. It sits above S/4HANA or ECC, takes master and transaction data from it, and sends plans back for execution. It matters when planning is genuinely complex, and it is SAP's successor for most APO planning as SAP SCM maintenance winds down. This guide is for supply chain directors, CFOs and programme leads deciding whether IBP is worth it and how to avoid the usual failure. That failure is rarely technical. Planners do not trust the outputs, so they keep their spreadsheets.

I have seen supply chain teams go live with SAP IBP and still plan in silos. Forecasting sat in one spreadsheet, production planning in another, and logistics was making calls based on yesterday's numbers. IBP was deployed. The integration to S/4HANA was running. The dashboards were populated.

The planners did not trust what they showed and kept their own models. I worked with one company where planners secretly maintained their old spreadsheets alongside IBP for months. The system was there, but the way of working had not changed.

## What SAP IBP covers

IBP is a cloud service built on SAP HANA. Its applications cover:

1. **Sales and operations planning (S&OP)**: the coordination layer where sales, marketing, supply chain and finance agree one number for demand, supply and financial impact
2. **Demand**: statistical and machine-learning forecasts enriched with promotions and commercial input, with version and consensus management and forecast accuracy tracking
3. **Inventory**: target stock by product and location from service levels, demand variability and lead times, including multi-echelon networks
4. **Response and supply**: constrained supply plans across the network, plus order-based planning to respond when plan and reality diverge
5. **Demand-driven replenishment**: buffer positioning based on the DDMRP method

SAP Supply Chain Control Tower, for visibility and alerts across the whole chain, integrates natively with IBP. [SAP's application overview](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_INTEGRATED_BUSINESS_PLANNING/6b0a6820ebf94ff4a15d68af6db7745b/b48f855725308a1be10000000a44147b.html) lists the current scope.

Joule has been generally available in IBP since release 2502 (January 2025) under a separate licence. It answers questions from SAP's IBP documentation and your own grounded documents, opens the right apps, runs master data health checks and schedules and monitors jobs. Useful, but it does not plan for you.

## How IBP fits with S/4HANA

IBP is not a module of S/4HANA. It is a separate cloud product with its own subscription, including for RISE with SAP customers.

S/4HANA runs operational execution: MRP, production orders, shop floor schedules and confirmations. IBP works at the tactical and strategic level: monthly and weekly demand and supply planning, S&OP and inventory strategy. My [SAP PP guide](/sap-pp-production-planning/) covers the S/4HANA side, including why classic SOP in S/4HANA is compatibility scope with IBP as its named successor.

The integration runs both ways. Master data (products, locations, resources) and transaction data (sales history, open orders, stock) flow from S/4HANA to IBP. Plans flow back to drive execution. There are two main routes:

1. **Time-series planning** (S&OP, demand, inventory) integrates through SAP Cloud Integration for data services, with an add-on in S/4HANA or ECC to simplify extraction.
2. **Order-based planning** (response and supply) uses [real-time integration](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_INTEGRATED_BUSINESS_PLANNING/68fa1e86fe6f41d98421d1ce13a08a9f/a4ed76f3d902423f89cb5899b56cb5a0.html) with ECC or S/4HANA, built on the Core Interface (CIF).

This is where data governance decides the outcome. If material masters carry wrong lead times, missing planning parameters or incorrect plant assignments, IBP will plan on those errors. The output is technically correct and operationally wrong.

## Do you need IBP?

Use this table as a first test before anyone signs a subscription.

| Signal | Points to IBP | Points to S/4HANA planning alone |
|---|---|---|
| Sales channels | Retail, direct and export with different lead times and service levels | One main channel |
| Demand pattern | Seasonal or promotion-driven | Stable |
| Distribution network | Factory to regional and local warehouses | Single site or simple network |
| Supply constraints | Real capacity or supplier limits that need optimisation | Capacity rarely binds |
| S&OP maturity | Monthly cycle exists but runs on separate spreadsheets | No S&OP process yet |
| Product range | Many SKUs and locations | Limited SKU count |

If most of your answers sit in the right-hand column, S/4HANA MRP and production planning cover the operational need, and the extra cost of IBP may not pay back. If you have no S&OP process at all, design the process first. Software will not create one.

## Where IBP implementations go wrong

### Data quality not fixed before configuration

Teams that configure before fixing data produce early plans that do not match reality: forecasts ignoring current lead times, supply plans ignoring real capacity, inventory targets built on incomplete history. Planners respond by ignoring the system. By the time the data is fixed, ignoring IBP has become a habit, and changing that habit is harder than fixing the data would have been.

The fix: assess S/4HANA master data against IBP's requirements before configuration starts, close the gaps, and check that outputs are plausible before planners are asked to rely on them. My piece on [why SAP data migration fails](/why-sap-data-migration-fails-and-how-to-fix-it/) covers how to run that assessment.

### S&OP not redesigned around IBP

In most organisations S&OP is a series of meetings where each function presents its own numbers. Put IBP under that and you get new outputs from the same disconnected process. Effective S&OP needs a fixed data submission cycle, a defined path for resolving demand and supply gaps, and executive decisions that are binding rather than advisory. The software makes that process easier to run. It cannot run a process that does not exist.

### Integration scope left vague

Specify the IBP integration like any other: which master data objects, which transactions flow back to S/4HANA and when, and who reconciles when the two systems disagree. Integrations described at concept level and left to the technical team surface as gaps in testing or, worse, in production.

### Adoption treated as training

Training teaches people the screens. It does not create trust. Trust comes from accuracy: planners who see IBP forecasts beat their own models will switch. Planners who see regular misses will not, and the misses usually come from poor data, untuned statistical models or business exceptions nobody fed in. Show accurate outputs before you ask planners to commit.

## Readiness checklist

Before the IBP project starts, confirm these in order:

1. A named owner for each master data domain IBP will consume
2. A data quality assessment against IBP's requirements, with gaps closed or scheduled
3. A designed S&OP cycle: calendar, submission deadlines, decision rights and escalation path
4. An integration specification listing objects, direction, frequency and reconciliation owner
5. A forecast accuracy baseline from current methods, so you can show IBP doing better
6. A parallel-run period in which planners compare IBP output with their own models

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is SAP IBP and what does it do?</summary>

SAP Integrated Business Planning is SAP's cloud supply chain planning suite. It covers sales and operations planning, demand forecasting, inventory optimisation, response and supply planning, and demand-driven replenishment. Instead of each function planning on its own spreadsheet, IBP gives them a shared dataset and process. It plans; S/4HANA or ECC executes.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the applications in SAP IBP?</summary>

SAP IBP for sales and operations, SAP IBP for demand, SAP IBP for inventory, SAP IBP for response and supply, and demand-driven replenishment. SAP Supply Chain Control Tower integrates natively for visibility and alerts. Most companies start with S&OP and demand, then add inventory and supply.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is SAP IBP part of S/4HANA?</summary>

No. IBP is a separate cloud product with its own subscription, and it is not included in a base RISE with SAP contract. S/4HANA handles operational execution such as MRP and production orders. IBP handles tactical and strategic planning. Time-series data integrates through SAP Cloud Integration for data services, and order-based planning uses real-time integration built on the Core Interface (CIF).

</details>

<details>
<summary>What data does SAP IBP need from S/4HANA?</summary>

Master data: products with planning parameters and lead times, locations, resources and capacities, and the network of what ships from where. Transaction data: sales history, open sales orders, stock by location, and open production and purchase orders. The quality of that data sets the quality of the plans. Wrong lead times give wrong timing; inconsistent locations leave holes in the network plan.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How long does an SAP IBP implementation take?</summary>

Implementation typically takes six to 12 months, and I would not trust anyone promising faster. A full implementation across all areas, with complex network design and multi-echelon inventory optimisation, can take 12 to 18 months. The most reliable cause of overruns is unscoped data work, for example discovering mid-project that many products have no lead time in the material master.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between SAP APO and SAP IBP?</summary>

SAP APO (Advanced Planner and Optimizer) is the on-premise planning component of SAP SCM 7.0, whose mainstream maintenance ends in 2027, with optional extended maintenance to 2030. IBP is SAP's cloud successor for most APO planning; detailed production scheduling moves to embedded PP/DS in S/4HANA. SAP's Readiness Check for supply chain helps scope the move. Treat it as a planning redesign, not a lift and shift.

</details>
