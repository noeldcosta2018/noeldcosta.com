SAP Activate is not just a methodology. It is a structured set of phase-by-phase templates that give an implementation team a working foundation instead of a blank page.

The pattern I have seen consistently across ECC and S/4HANA programmes in manufacturing, retail, and financial services: teams that follow the templates move faster and catch problems earlier. Teams that treat the templates as optional documentation discover mid-project that the decisions they skipped codifying are now the source of every scope dispute.

The six phases are Discover, Prepare, Explore, Realize, Deploy, and Run. This guide covers the templates that matter most in each phase and where teams underuse them.

## Understanding SAP Activate

SAP Activate combines pre-configured best practices, guided configuration, and Agile project management in one framework. You are not reinventing the wheel. You are standing on templates refined across thousands of implementations.

The phase sequence is not optional. I worked with a retail client who tried to skip Explore and move straight to Realize. They spent three months building against incomplete requirements and had to redo significant configuration. Every quality gate exists for a reason.

When adapting templates, keep around 80% of the standard structure and change only what genuinely reflects your specific context: industry requirements, regulatory controls, regional specifics. Changing everything defeats the purpose.

## Prepare phase templates

The Prepare phase templates are the foundation of the entire implementation. Projects that skip this groundwork pay for it in Explore and Realize.

### Project scoping template

Defines exactly what the project includes and what it does not. When someone tries to add scope three months in (and they will), this document is the reference point.

| Section | Details |
|---|---|
| Project title | SAP S/4HANA Finance implementation |
| Project sponsor | CFO / Group Finance Director |
| Project manager | Senior PM name and role |
| Background | Current state description and driver for change |
| Objectives | Reduce close cycle from 14 days to 5; eliminate manual reconciliations |
| In scope | FI/CO, MM/SD integration, data migration, UAT, go-live |
| Out of scope | HR modules, legacy report migration, third-party integrations beyond ERP |
| Assumptions | Executive sponsor available for monthly SteerCo; test data agreed by Week 6 |
| Constraints | Fixed go-live date; internal resources only for configuration |
| Deliverables | Configured system, test plans, cutover plan, training materials |
| Timeline | Prepare: Weeks 1-4; Explore: Weeks 5-10; Realize: Weeks 11-26 |
| Approval | Project sponsor and PMO sign-off required before Explore begins |

### Business case template

Walks through the cost-benefit analysis in a format the finance team can read. I have had clients get programme approval on the first submission using this structure because the numbers are clear and the assumptions are documented.

| Section | Details |
|---|---|
| Project name | S/4HANA transformation programme |
| Business owner | CFO / programme director |
| Executive summary | Why the programme is needed; what changes and what stays the same |
| Strategic alignment | Links to digital strategy, regulatory requirements, or operating model targets |
| Problem statement | Specific operational issues being addressed (close cycle length, manual workarounds, system age) |
| Proposed solution | SAP Activate greenfield / brownfield / selective with scope summary |
| Benefits | Quantified: days off close cycle, FTE savings, error rate reduction, audit risk reduction |
| Cost estimate | Implementation cost, licence cost, internal resource cost, contingency |

| Risks | Top three risks with probability and impact |
| Recommendation | Proceed / conditional proceed / defer with rationale |

## Explore phase templates

Once the groundwork is in place, Explore is where the SAP implementation takes shape. These templates surface the gap between what SAP does out of the box and what the business actually needs.

| REQ-002 | Purchase order approval via Fiori | Procurement governance | MM-PO, Fiori approval app | TC-003, TC-004 |
| REQ-003 | Intercompany billing automation | Reduce IC reconciliation cycle | SD-IV, FI-AP integration | TC-010 |

### Fit-gap analysis template

Shows clearly where standard SAP functionality meets your needs and where customisation is required. On a manufacturing ECC-to-S/4HANA migration, the fit-gap workshop reduced planned enhancements by 35% because teams realised standard S/4HANA functionality had evolved to cover cases they were custom-coding in ECC.

| Req ID | Requirement | Current capability | Fit / Gap | Remediation approach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| REQ-001 | Automated close journals | Partially automated via recurring entries | Fit | Configure recurring document templates |
| REQ-002 | Fiori PO approval | Not available in ECC | Gap | S/4HANA Fiori app configuration |
| REQ-003 | Intercompany billing | Manual invoice process | Gap | SD-IV configuration and FI integration |
| REQ-004 | Batch job monitoring | SM37 available but no dashboard | Fit | Standard Fiori tile for batch monitoring |
| REQ-005 | Supplier self-service | No portal | Gap | Ariba integration or SRM configuration |
| REQ-006 | GDPR archiving | No archiving configured | Gap | ILM policy configuration |
| REQ-007 | Real-time cost centre reporting | Monthly BW loads | Gap | SAC or BW/4HANA real-time integration |
| REQ-008 | 500 concurrent users | System supports 300 (tested) | Gap | HANA sizing review and infrastructure uplift |

## Realize phase templates

This is the build phase. The templates here are the audit trail for every configuration decision, every development, and every test result.

### Configuration tracking template

Records every system change: who made it, why, and which transport it went in. When something breaks later, you trace the issue in minutes rather than days.

1. Configuration ID: [e.g., MM-CONF-001]
2. Module: [e.g., MM]
3. SPRO path: [Exact path in SAP IMG]
4. Description: [Purpose of this configuration]
5. Configured by: [Name / Role]
6. Configuration date: [YYYY-MM-DD]
7. Transport request number: [e.g., DEVK900123]
8. Object type: Table / View / Transaction
9. Configuration object: [e.g., Table T161 - PO Document Types]
10. Key values changed: [Before and after]
11. Functional impact: [Business process affected]
12. Test cases linked: [Test Case ID]
13. Validation status: Pending / Validated / Failed
14. Approval status: Pending / Approved / Rejected

### Testing strategy template

Maps all testing plans in one place: who tests what, when, in which environment, and to what standard.

| Section | Details |
|---|---|
| Testing objectives | Validate business processes end-to-end; identify defects before go-live; confirm data migration accuracy |
| Test scope | Functional, integration, regression, performance, UAT across all in-scope modules |
| Out of scope | Penetration testing (InfoSec team); legacy report migration |

### Data migration planning template

Data migration is consistently the highest-risk workstream. This template breaks it into steps that expose data quality issues before cutover rather than during it.

| Section | Details |
|---|---|
| Scope | Customer master, vendor master, open items, material master, stock balances, cost centre hierarchies |
| Source systems | ECC 6.0 EHP 7 (primary); legacy HR system (employee cost centre assignments) |
| Target system | S/4HANA 2023 on SAP HANA |
| Data mapping | Legacy BP numbers to S/4HANA Business Partner; legacy cost centres to S/4HANA cost centres with new hierarchy |
| Transformation rules | Normalise names to title case; remove invalid bank accounts; consolidate duplicate BP records |
| Migration tools | SAP Migration Cockpit (primary); Python scripts for pre-processing; LSMW for legacy objects |
| Load strategy | Phase 1: dry run on QA; Phase 2: delta migration and reconciliation; Phase 3: production cutover |
| Validation approach | Record count checks source-to-target; 10% random sampling; balance reconciliation reports |
| Rollback plan | Pre-cutover backup; rollback script clears migrated records by timestamp; legacy system on standby 48h |

## Deploy phase templates

The Deploy phase is when you go live. These templates turn a chaotic weekend into a managed event.

### Cutover planning template

Maps the blackout window hour by hour. Every task, every owner, every start time. A client found a major configuration issue two days before go-live because this checklist exposed it during the pre-cutover review. Without the template, it would have surfaced at 2 AM on the night.

## Quality gates

Quality gates stop a problem in one phase from becoming expensive rework in the next.

Executive sign-off before go-live should not be a rubber stamp. On one programme I ran, the go-live review caught a finance close configuration issue that would have generated incorrect opening balances for all 25 entities. Two extra days of testing. The alternative was weeks of corrective postings post go-live.

Clear pass/fail criteria at each gate. "95% of regression tests must pass" or "all FI integration scenarios are green." These give you a defensible basis to hold the line when the business is pushing to launch on a specific date regardless of quality.

A quality gate on one programme stopped us when integration test pass rate was at 74%. We held, fixed the issues, and went live two weeks later. Post-go-live support tickets in the first month were half what comparable programmes typically see.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is the SAP Activate methodology?</summary>

SAP Activate is SAP's official implementation methodology for S/4HANA and other SAP products. It combines pre-configured best practices, guided configuration, and Agile project management into a six-phase framework: Discover, Prepare, Explore, Realize, Deploy, and Run.

Each phase has specific templates, deliverables, and quality gates designed to reduce implementation risk and accelerate delivery. The methodology is publicly available through SAP Activate Roadmap Viewer, which provides phase-by-phase task lists and deliverable templates for different deployment scenarios (RISE with SAP, greenfield, brownfield, selective data transition).

</details>

<details>
<summary>Which SAP Activate phase has the most important templates?</summary>

The Prepare phase is where the most critical templates sit, and where they are most often skipped.

The scoping document, business case, and stakeholder identification matrix set the foundation for every decision that follows. Skipping these in favour of getting straight to configuration is the single most common cause of scope disputes and rework in the Realize phase.

The Explore phase comes second. The fit-gap analysis and requirements mapping templates determine what gets built. Gaps in these documents surface as UAT defects six months later, at which point fixing them is significantly more expensive than it would have been in week 3 of Explore.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can you customise SAP Activate templates?</summary>

Yes. SAP Activate templates are designed to be adapted to your industry, regulatory environment, and delivery model.

In practice, keep around 80% of the standard structure and change only what is genuinely specific to your context: pharmaceutical validation requirements, public sector procurement rules, financial services SOX controls. These additions belong in the template set from the start of Prepare, not retrofitted in Deploy.

What you should not customise: the quality gate structure, the phase sequence, and the mandatory artefacts (scoping document, business case, go-live readiness assessment). These exist because the implementation lessons they encode are consistent across industries and project sizes.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do SAP Activate templates work for both greenfield and brownfield?</summary>

Yes. The templates apply to both approaches. The main difference is in the Explore phase.

A brownfield migration carries existing configuration forward from ECC. The fit-gap analysis focuses more on what needs to change to meet clean-core requirements, what customisation can be replaced by standard S/4HANA functionality, and what data and process cleanup is required before the conversion.

A greenfield deployment starts from SAP Best Practices and adapts from there. The requirements mapping and fit-gap templates focus on confirming which standard processes meet business needs and where genuine gaps require configuration or extension work.

The cutover planning template also differs materially between the two. A brownfield system conversion has a different cutover sequence from a greenfield go-live with full data migration.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the purpose of the fit-gap analysis template?</summary>

The fit-gap analysis compares business requirements against standard SAP functionality. It identifies where SAP covers the need out of the box (fit), where configuration is required (partial fit), and where custom development is needed (gap).

Running this analysis properly before Realize begins is the best way to reduce custom code. Custom code is what makes SAP implementations expensive and fragile: it requires maintenance across every SAP upgrade, creates technical debt that accumulates with each release, and often solves problems that SAP standard functionality has since addressed.

A well-run fit-gap workshop consistently produces fewer enhancements than the initial requirements list suggests. Teams discover that SAP has evolved to cover cases they were customising in ECC, and that standard processes they assumed would not work for their business actually do with minor configuration.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How detailed should a cutover plan be?</summary>

Hour-by-hour at minimum. Minute-by-minute for the blackout window.

A cutover plan that lists tasks without start times, owners, and explicit rollback triggers is not a cutover plan. It is a high-level checklist. The cutover weekend is the highest-risk event in the entire programme, and the decisions made during it are made by people who have been awake for 20 hours under significant pressure.

The plan needs to be specific enough that any team member can pick it up and know exactly what to do next. Rollback criteria should be agreed before the cutover begins: what conditions would trigger the decision to return to the legacy system, who makes that decision, and by what time does the decision need to be made. Rollback decisions made under pressure at 4 AM without pre-agreed criteria are where post-go-live disasters originate.

</details>
