There is no single best ERP for a small business, but there is a short list worth your time. Mine has six systems: SAP Business One, SAP Business ByDesign, Oracle NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, Odoo and Zoho, and which one fits depends on the business.

The most common mistake I see small firms make is going for the cheapest price. A cheap licence that does not fit your processes costs more by year two than a dearer one that does.

This page is the software comparison: what each system is, who it suits, how it is deployed and how the vendor charges. If you want the operational side first, when it is time for ERP and the selection mistakes that hurt operations, read my guide to the [best ERP for small business operations](/best-erp-for-small-business-operations/).

## Why small businesses start looking at ERP

Running a small business gets harder once the spreadsheets stop adding up. Finance numbers do not match what sales reports. Orders fall through the cracks.

That is usually when owners start looking at ERP: not because they want another tool, but because the current setup leaves too many blind spots.

Most comparison sites list the same vendors in polished tables and leave out the trade-offs. I have seen teams abandon systems after two months because every invoice took five steps. That detail rarely makes it into the glossy reviews.

I also remember one client who thought an invoicing application and a basic inventory tool were enough. Within months, it was spending more time fixing mistakes than serving customers.

### 1. SAP Business One

SAP's ERP for small businesses, sold, implemented and supported through SAP partners. It covers finance, sales, purchasing, inventory and light production in one system. It runs on Microsoft SQL Server or SAP HANA, either on your own server or in the cloud.

The partner sets the price for your country. In my experience, a typical SAP Business One project costs $25K to $150K or more and takes 90 to 150 days. My [SAP Business One price guide](/sap-business-one-price-guide/) breaks the cost down by licence type and partner work.

Version 10.0 is in [mainstream maintenance until 31 December 2028](https://support.sap.com/en/offerings-programs/support-small-medium-enterprises/business-one/maintenance.html), and SAP has said version 11 will follow. That matters when you compare a perpetual licence with a cloud fee: plan for an upgrade inside your first five years.

**Who it fits:** a single company or a small group that wants structured finance and operations, an SAP name behind it, and a local partner.

**Watch for:** partner quality varies a great deal, and buying full licences for people who only need limited access inflates the bill. Heavy customisation makes later upgrades harder.

### 2. SAP Business ByDesign

SAP's cloud ERP for fast-growing midsize companies. SAP runs it as software as a service, with prebuilt processes across finance, sales, purchasing, projects and supply chain. It handles several companies, currencies and sets of books in one tenant, which is why subsidiaries of larger groups use it.

It is a bigger step than Business One. You adopt SAP's processes rather than shaping the system around yours, and the subscription is quoted, not published.

One point to check before you sign. SAP's own ByDesign pages now describe a path to migrate from ByDesign to SAP Cloud ERP. Ask SAP or your partner for the product roadmap in writing, so you know how long your choice will be supported.

**Who it fits:** a growing company, or a subsidiary of a larger group, that needs multi-company accounting and project work in a cloud system SAP runs.

**Watch for:** limited room to customise, and a roadmap you should confirm before committing for several years.

### 3. Oracle NetSuite

Full cloud ERP covering finance, inventory, order management, CRM and e-commerce. Strong for businesses managing several entities or growing fast. Consolidating across subsidiaries without juggling spreadsheets is one of its real strengths.

NetSuite does not publish a price list. Its own pages describe an annual licence built from three parts: the core platform, optional modules and the number of users, with a one-time implementation fee on top. Accounting, inventory, order management and tax sit in the core platform; other modules are licensed separately.

The base platform is solid, but reports and modules that buyers assume are standard are often add-ons, and the add-ons add up. Ask for a written three-year price that names every module you will use.

**Who it fits:** multi-entity, fast-growing companies in distribution, retail, e-commerce and services.

**Watch for:** add-on creep, and customisation that needs SuiteScript skills. Without internal IT, maintenance costs are real.

### 4. Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central

Finance, sales, purchasing, inventory and projects, with close links to Outlook, Excel and Teams. Adoption is easier when the team already lives in Microsoft tools.

Microsoft is the only vendor on this list that publishes a firm list price for its main plans. Essentials is $80 per user per month and Premium, which adds service management and manufacturing, is $110, both billed yearly. Team Members, for people who only read data, approve workflows or update a few records, cost $8.

Copilot is included in the online plans, and [Microsoft states](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/features-not-implemented-on-premises) it is not available on-premises. Agents built in Copilot Studio are charged separately through Copilot credits.

Do not plan to roll it out without an implementation partner. Teams that assume it will plug in without configuration lose months and bring a partner in later to redesign the workflows anyway.

**Who it fits:** businesses standardised on Microsoft that want ERP without extra middleware.

**Watch for:** limited built-in industry depth, and partner quality that varies widely.

### 5. Odoo

Modular and open source at its core: start with a few apps such as CRM and invoicing and add more later. Popular with e-commerce brands, small manufacturers and service businesses with tight budgets.

Odoo's pricing page offers one app free for unlimited users, then Standard and Custom plans charged per user per month. Custom adds Studio, multi-company and external API access, and is the plan you need for Odoo.sh or self-hosting. Initial users get a discounted rate for the first 12 months, so check the renewal price, and prices differ by country.

The core risk is dependency. If your whole operation runs on custom Odoo code and the main developer leaves, even small updates become projects. Custom code on Odoo is fragile, and upgrades break things if they are not managed carefully.

**Who it fits:** businesses with development capacity that want flexibility over out-of-the-box depth.

**Watch for:** the price after the introductory year, and custom code with no plan for maintaining it.

### 6. Zoho

Zoho One bundles finance, CRM, inventory, HR and projects in one subscription. Zoho licenses it per user per month on annual terms, in two ways: an all-employee plan that requires a licence for everyone on payroll, or a flexible plan that licenses only the people who use it. There is a free trial of the full suite.

The interface is clean and users adapt quickly. It works until the business becomes operationally complex. When you need deep customisation, industry logic or complex multi-entity accounting, you start to outgrow it.

**Who it fits:** startups and small service or retail businesses that need one system without an enterprise budget.

**Watch for:** the point where you need manufacturing, complex inventory or group consolidation. Plan the move before you reach it.

## How to choose without buying on price

The cheapest quote wins too many small-business ERP decisions. It is the most common mistake I see. The licence is the visible number; partner fees, add-on modules, training, support and rework after go-live are the ones that decide the real cost.

The wrong way is to pick the system a competitor uses, or buy for features you might need in three years. The right way is to find your biggest operational gap today, demo the system with your own data, and involve the people who will use it daily, not only IT or procurement.

Before you sign, run this checklist:

1. Can it run your three most common transactions in a demo using your own data?
2. What is the written three-year total: subscription, add-ons, implementation, training and support?
3. Which modules you need are priced separately, and what happens to the price at renewal?
4. Who will configure and maintain it, and what happens if that person leaves?
5. How long will the vendor support this product or version, in writing?
6. Can you get your data out in a usable format if you switch later?

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is the best ERP software for a small business?</summary>

There is no single best one. My shortlist is SAP Business One, SAP Business ByDesign, Oracle NetSuite, Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, Odoo and Zoho. Which one fits depends on your industry, how many companies you run, the tools you already use and who will maintain the system.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the cheapest ERP for a small business?</summary>

Odoo has a free one-app plan and low per-user plans, and Zoho One bundles a full suite in one subscription. Do not choose on the lowest price alone. It is the most common mistake I see small firms make, because a cheap system that does not fit costs more once partner work, add-ons and rework are counted.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How much does SAP Business One cost for a small business?</summary>

SAP sells Business One through partners, who quote by country, licence type and user count. In my experience a typical project costs $25K to $150K or more and takes 90 to 150 days. My SAP Business One price guide breaks the cost down further.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between SAP Business One and SAP Business ByDesign?</summary>

Business One is for small companies and can run on-premise or in the cloud through a partner. ByDesign is a cloud ERP that SAP runs, aimed at fast-growing midsize companies and subsidiaries that need multi-company accounting. Ask for ByDesign's roadmap in writing before you commit.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is NetSuite or Business Central better for a small business?</summary>

Business Central suits firms already on Microsoft 365 and publishes its per-user price. NetSuite suits multi-entity, fast-growing companies that want consolidation, order management and e-commerce in one cloud platform, and it is quoted by module and user. Demo both with your own transactions before deciding.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do small businesses need an implementation partner?</summary>

For most of these systems, yes. SAP Business One and Business Central are sold and configured through partners, and NetSuite charges a one-time implementation fee. Ask for references from businesses of your size and industry, not the vendor's showcase clients.

</details>
