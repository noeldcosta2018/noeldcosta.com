The best ERP for a small business depends on what is breaking. Finance-led firms usually fit Sage Intacct. Businesses already on Microsoft fit Dynamics 365 Business Central. Multi-entity, fast-growing companies look at NetSuite or Acumatica. Tight budgets point to Zoho One, Odoo or ERPNext, and small manufacturers to Katana. On the SAP side, SAP Business One serves small businesses and SAP GROW serves growing companies that want the wider SAP portfolio. This guide is for owners, finance leads and operations managers choosing their first real ERP. It compares ten systems, explains the pricing models, and ends with a shortlisting checklist. Start by naming your biggest operational gap today.

Running a small business gets harder once the spreadsheets stop adding up. Finance numbers do not match what sales reports. Orders fall through the cracks. That is usually when owners start looking at ERP: not because they want another tool, but because the current setup leaves too many blind spots.

Most comparison sites list the same vendors in polished tables and leave out the trade-offs. I have seen teams abandon systems after two months because every invoice took five steps. That detail rarely makes it into the glossy reviews. I also remember one client who thought an invoicing application and a basic inventory tool were enough. Within months, it was spending more time fixing mistakes than serving customers.

### 1. Oracle NetSuite

Full-featured cloud ERP covering finance, inventory, CRM and e-commerce. Strong for businesses managing several entities or growing fast. Consolidating across subsidiaries without juggling spreadsheets is one of its real strengths.

The base platform is solid, but reports and modules that buyers assume are standard are often add-ons, and the add-ons add up. The year-three total can run 40 to 60 percent above the year-one quote, so ask for a three-year price.

**Watch for:** no free trial, add-on creep, and customisation that needs SuiteScript skills. Without internal IT, maintenance costs are real.

### 2. SAP Business One

SAP's ERP for small businesses, sold and supported through partners. It covers finance, sales, purchasing, inventory and light production, and runs on SAP HANA or Microsoft SQL Server, on your own server or in a partner's cloud. The partner sets the price for your country.

It suits small companies that want SAP discipline without an S/4HANA programme. The usual mistake is buying full Professional licences for everyone when most people need a cheaper Limited licence. My [SAP Business One pricing guide](/sap-business-one-price-guide/) breaks down the costs.

**Watch for:** partner quality varies, and heavy customisation makes later moves harder.

### 3. SAP GROW (SAP's public cloud ERP)

[SAP GROW](https://www.sap.com/products/erp/grow/what-is-sap-grow.html) is SAP's route for companies new to SAP, built on S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition (now sold as SAP Cloud ERP) with SAP SuccessFactors for HR. Finance, procurement, supply chain and sales come preconfigured. Clean core is enforced by design: there is no custom code in the core, and extensions go through SAP BTP or released APIs.

It is a new implementation, not a conversion of an old system. SAP's fixed-scope GROW Fast offering, launched in early 2026, targets go-live in two to four months. A typical public cloud project takes five to seven months. Joule is embedded, and SAP Cloud ALM comes with the subscription's support entitlement.

The trade-off is rigidity. If your processes do not fit the standard model, you will hit walls. Adjusting tax treatments across regions can need partner support for configuration that looks minor.

**Best for:** growing businesses that can adopt standard SAP processes and want a path to Ariba, SuccessFactors or SAP Analytics Cloud later.

### 4. Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central

Finance, inventory, projects and purchasing, with close links to Outlook, Excel and Teams. Adoption is easier when the team already lives in Microsoft tools. Copilot is included, and newer agents for sales orders and payables use separately sold Copilot credits. Microsoft lists [Essentials at $80 and Premium at $110](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics-365/products/business-central/pricing) per user per month on annual billing.

Do not plan to roll it out without an implementation partner. Teams that assume it will plug in without configuration lose months and bring a partner in later to redesign the workflows anyway.

**Watch for:** limited built-in industry depth, and partner quality that varies widely.

### 5. Odoo

Open-source and modular: start with a few apps such as CRM and invoicing and add more later. Popular with e-commerce brands, small manufacturers and service businesses with tight budgets and developer capacity. The Community edition is free to self-host. Paid plans are per user, with introductory rates that rise at renewal and prices that differ by country.

The core risk is dependency. If your whole operation runs on custom Odoo code and the main developer leaves, even small updates become projects. Custom code on Odoo is fragile, and upgrades break things if they are not managed carefully.

**Best for:** businesses with development capacity that want flexibility over out-of-the-box depth.

### 6. Sage Intacct

Finance-first cloud ERP for organisations where accounting accuracy and compliance matter more than operational breadth. Strong in professional services and nonprofits, with multi-entity consolidation and audit trails built in.

It is not the right system for manufacturing, heavy inventory or supply chain. It excels at giving finance a system that handles complex accounting without operational modules attached.

**Best for:** professional services firms, nonprofits and businesses with complex revenue recognition.

### 7. Acumatica

Cloud ERP priced on the resources you consume rather than per user, which helps when headcount fluctuates or grows. Strong in construction, distribution, field services and manufacturing.

Once configured it works well, but onboarding needs detailed business input on dispatch, inventory and cost tracking. The system will not configure itself.

**Best for:** operations-heavy businesses expecting headcount growth.

### 8. ERPNext

Fully open-source, covering accounting, HR, manufacturing, CRM, inventory and projects. The software is free; you pay for hosting, whether on your own servers or a managed cloud, and for support.

Self-hosting needs DevOps capability. Without it, you either pay a partner for maintenance or end up on an unmaintained version.

**Best for:** cost-sensitive businesses with internal technical capability that want control of their stack.

### 9. Zoho One

An all-in-one suite covering finance, CRM, inventory, HR and projects in one subscription. The all-employee plan requires a licence for everyone on payroll; the flexible plan licenses only the people who use it. The interface is clean and users adapt quickly, and there is a free trial.

It works until the business becomes operationally complex. When you need deep customisation, industry logic or complex multi-entity accounting, you start to outgrow it.

**Best for:** startups and small service or retail businesses that need one system without an enterprise budget.

### 10. Katana

Built for small manufacturers: make-to-order, D2C brands and workshops. A sales order updates materials, schedules production and adjusts inventory. There is no HR or payroll, only what a small manufacturer needs. It integrates with Shopify, Xero, QuickBooks Online and similar tools. Its current pricing is a base plan plus charges for sales orders delivered, extra locations and manufacturing add-ons, so model your real volume before you sign.

**Best for:** small manufacturers with make-to-order or D2C workflows that want a dedicated production system.

A note for QuickBooks users: Intuit [discontinued QuickBooks Commerce](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/other-questions/commerce-sunsetting/00/1243491) as a standalone product on 31 August 2023. If you need inventory and order management beyond QuickBooks Online, look at Katana or a full ERP above.

## Where SAP fits for a small or growing business

SAP has two products in this space, and they solve different problems.

**SAP Business One** is for small businesses, typically a single company or a small group, that want structured finance and operations at a price a small firm can carry. It is installed and supported by a partner.

**SAP GROW** is for growing companies that want SAP's public cloud ERP from the start, accept standard processes, and expect to add more SAP products as they scale. It is a bigger commitment, with mandatory cloud upgrades and no custom code in the core.

Where NetSuite or Business Central wins: organisations that need more flexibility on day one, or that will never grow into the wider SAP portfolio. Where GROW loses: businesses whose processes cannot live with the standard model. Those end up on a private cloud ERP at a much higher budget. ## How to choose

The wrong way: pick the system a competitor uses, or buy for features you might need in three years. The right way: find your biggest operational gap today, trial the system with your own data, and involve the people who will use it daily, not only IT or procurement.

Most small businesses fall into one of four profiles:

1. **Finance-led:** Sage Intacct
2. **Operations-heavy with growth plans:** Acumatica, NetSuite or SAP GROW
3. **Already on Microsoft:** Dynamics 365 Business Central
4. **Budget-constrained:** Zoho One, ERPNext, Odoo or SAP Business One with a starter licence

Before you sign, run this shortlisting checklist:

1. Can it run your three most common transactions in a demo using your own data?
2. What is the written three-year total: subscription, add-ons, implementation, training and support?
3. Which modules you need are priced separately?
4. Who will configure and maintain it, and what happens if that person leaves?
5. Can you get your data out in a usable format if you switch later?

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is an affordable ERP for small business?</summary>

Odoo (free Community edition, low-cost paid plans), ERPNext (free software, pay for hosting) and Zoho One (one subscription for the full suite) keep entry costs lowest. Affordable has a ceiling: once you need complex customisation, multi-entity accounting or industry logic, the cheaper options need paid development to extend.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do small businesses need ERP?</summary>

Yes, in three situations. You run operations across several systems and spend hours reconciling them. Onboarding takes weeks because workflows are inconsistent. Or inventory, sales and finance give different numbers for the same question. ERP fixes coordination failures. It does not replace good processes; it makes consistent processes possible as you grow.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the best ERP for a small manufacturer?</summary>

Katana for small make-to-order or D2C manufacturers that want a focused, easy system. Acumatica or Epicor Kinetic for manufacturers needing deeper production scheduling or multi-warehouse inventory. SAP Business One or SAP GROW if you want SAP as the business grows. The test: can it handle your bills of materials, production scheduling and lot traceability? If it cannot trace a defective part back to the production run, it is not adequate for regulated manufacturing.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How much should a small business budget for ERP?</summary>

Plan the three-year total, not the annual subscription. Include implementation or setup (often one to two times the first-year licence for a properly configured system), customisation, training and annual support. Watch for modules that are functionally necessary but priced separately. Ask every vendor for a written three-year cost scenario before signing.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What ERP can a very small business or solo founder use?</summary>

ERPNext and Zoho's finance and inventory apps are the most accessible starting points, and Odoo's single free app covers one function for unlimited users. If accounting already runs on QuickBooks Online, add a connected inventory tool rather than switching. At this size, avoid anything that needs an implementation partner to configure.

</details>
