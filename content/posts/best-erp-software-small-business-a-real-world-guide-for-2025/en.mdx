Running a small business gets harder once the spreadsheets stop adding up. Finance numbers do not match what sales is reporting. Orders fall through the cracks. That is usually when owners start looking for ERP, not because they want another tool, but because the current setup leaves too many blind spots.

Most websites list the same vendors in polished comparison tables and leave out the real trade-offs. Across 21 SAP and non-SAP advisory engagements I have seen teams abandon systems after two months because every invoice took five steps. That detail rarely makes it into the glossy reviews.

This guide compares ten ERPs for small business as the 2026 commercial landscape stands. SAP GROW moved into the mid-market entry conversation in 2024-2025 and changed the shortlist for organisations 50 to 1,000 staff; the article closes with a section on how RISE, GROW, and the clean-core posture shape the SAP-side decision.

## The ten systems compared

| ERP | Best for | Deployment | Starting cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oracle NetSuite** | Multi-location, high-growth | Cloud only | $99–$150/user/mo |
| **SAP GROW** | Clean cloud entry, 50–500 employees | Cloud only | ~$90/user/mo |
| **Dynamics 365 Business Central** | Microsoft-stack businesses | Cloud / on-prem | $70/user/mo |
| **Odoo** | Budget, flexibility, developers on staff | Cloud / on-prem | Free (self-host) / $25/user/mo |
| **Sage Intacct** | Finance-led organisations, nonprofits | Cloud only | $250–$400/user/mo |
| **Acumatica** | Growing teams, consumption pricing | Cloud | Usage-based |

| **QuickBooks Commerce** | Existing QuickBooks users | Cloud only | $75/mo |
| **Katana ERP** | Small manufacturers, make-to-order | Cloud only | $179/mo base |

### 1. Oracle NetSuite

Full-featured ERP covering finance, inventory, CRM, and e-commerce in one connected system. 36,000+ installs. Strong for businesses managing multiple entities or rapid growth. The multi-entity setup is genuinely smooth: consolidating across subsidiaries without juggling spreadsheets.

A US consumer electronics company I advised in 2023 used NetSuite for retail and wholesale. Visibility was good, but they ended up paying extra for several reports they assumed were standard. That is a pattern: the base platform is solid, but the add-ons add up fast and the year-three total often runs 40 to 60 percent above the year-one quote.

**Strengths:** Unified suite, multi-entity, frequent updates, modern UI.

**Weaknesses:** No free trial, pricing escalates with add-ons, customisation requires SuiteScript expertise. For teams without internal IT, ongoing maintenance costs are real.

**Best for:** Mid-size businesses expecting complex growth: multi-location, multi-currency, e-commerce alongside wholesale.

### 2. SAP GROW (S/4HANA Cloud public edition)

SAP's cloud ERP for businesses that want structured processes without building from scratch. Runs on S/4HANA Cloud public edition, greenfield only. Target size: 50 to 1,000 staff. Clean-core posture is contractual, not optional.

Finance, procurement, supply chain, and sales processes are preconfigured. Go-live is typically 6 to 9 months. The trade-off is rigidity: if your processes do not fit the standard model, you will hit walls. Adjusting tax treatments across regions can require partner support for configurations that look minor.

Total programme cost typically runs $2M to $6M end-to-end including partner services. In 2026, GROW is increasingly the SAP-side answer for organisations evaluating NetSuite or Business Central and wanting a clean path to the broader SAP product family later (Joule for SAP Activate, SAP Cloud ALM, Ariba, SuccessFactors).

**Strengths:** Fast start, no infrastructure to manage, SAP-native integrations, included SAP customer success engagement, Joule and Cloud ALM bundled in the subscription.

**Weaknesses:** Greenfield only (cannot migrate legacy logic from ECC), clean-core constraint blocks deep customisation, learning curve with the SAP data model.

**Best for:** Growing businesses that want clean cloud ERP, can adapt their processes to standard SAP flows, and want a credible path to the broader SAP product family as they scale.

### 3. Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central

Finance, inventory, project management, and purchasing, with clean connections to Outlook, Excel, and Teams. Adoption is easier when teams already live in Microsoft tools. Copilot in Dynamics now drafts journal entries, sales orders, and report narratives, which is useful when finance teams already use Copilot in M365.

A US 70-person consulting firm in 2023 tried to roll it out without an implementation partner. Six months later, they brought one in to redesign workflows. After that, it started working, but they lost months assuming it would plug in without configuration. It will not.

**Strengths:** Microsoft ecosystem integration, Power BI reporting, cloud or on-prem option.

**Weaknesses:** Not preloaded with deep industry logic, partner quality varies significantly.

**Best for:** Businesses standardised on Microsoft who want ERP without switching ecosystems.

### 4. Odoo

Open-source and modular: start with a few apps (CRM, invoicing) and add more over time. Used heavily by e-commerce brands, small manufacturers, and service businesses with tight budgets and developer capacity.

The core risk is dependency. A European food D2C business I advised in 2024 built their entire customer service and delivery system on Odoo. It worked until their main developer left. Suddenly, even small updates became a project. Custom code on Odoo is fragile. Upgrades break things if not carefully managed.

**Strengths:** Low entry cost, highly flexible, large community.

**Weaknesses:** Needs in-house technical skills to maintain custom logic. Without that, you become dependent on external consultants for every change.

**Best for:** Businesses with development capacity who want flexibility over out-of-the-box depth.

### 5. Sage Intacct

Finance-first cloud ERP for organisations where financial accuracy and compliance take priority over operational breadth. Strong in professional services and nonprofits. GAAP compliance, multi-entity consolidation, and audit trails are built in.

It is not the right system if you need operational depth: manufacturing, heavy inventory, or supply chain. Where it excels is giving finance teams a system that handles complex accounting without requiring operational modules alongside it.

**Strengths:** Core accounting depth, strong audit controls, multi-entity.

**Weaknesses:** Limited non-financial modules, $250–$400/user/month makes it one of the more expensive options per seat.

**Best for:** Finance-led organisations: professional services firms, nonprofits, businesses with complex revenue recognition requirements.

### 6. Acumatica

Cloud ERP priced by consumption rather than per user, useful for teams where headcount fluctuates or grows. Strong in construction, distribution, field services, and manufacturing.

A European construction materials client adopted Acumatica in 2023 for dispatch, inventory, and cost tracking. Once configured, it worked well, but they almost abandoned it during onboarding because setup needed detailed business input. The system will not configure itself. You need to understand what matters to your operation and communicate that to the implementation partner.

**Strengths:** Unlimited users without re-licensing, operational depth, flexible customisation.

**Weaknesses:** More complex to configure than entry-level tools, less polished UI.

**Best for:** Growing businesses in operations-heavy industries where headcount will increase and you do not want to re-license.

### 7. ERPNext

Fully open-source. Covers accounting, HR, manufacturing, CRM, inventory, and projects. Free to self-host; cloud hosting starts around $50 per month. Strong developer community and active development.

The honest trade-off: self-hosting needs DevOps capability. If you do not have that internally, you either pay a partner for maintenance or you end up on an unmaintained version. Enterprise support comes at a premium.

**Strengths:** No licence cost, flexible, broad coverage.

**Weaknesses:** Self-hosting requires technical investment; not plug-and-play.

**Best for:** Cost-sensitive businesses with internal technical capability who want control over their ERP stack.

### 8. Zoho One

All-in-one suite at $30 per user per month, the most affordable full-featured option on this list. Finance, CRM, inventory, HR, and projects in one subscription. Interface is clean and users adapt quickly.

It works well until the business becomes operationally complex. Zoho is designed for simplicity. When you need deep customisation, industry-specific logic, or complex multi-entity accounting, you will start to outgrow it.

**Strengths:** Affordable, full-featured, easy onboarding, 30-day trial.

**Weaknesses:** Limited third-party integrations compared to competitors, advanced customisation requires scripting.

**Best for:** Startups and small businesses in services or retail who need a unified system without enterprise-level budget.

### 9. QuickBooks Commerce

Not a full ERP: it is an inventory and order management layer built on top of QuickBooks Online. Adds product management, order tracking, and stock visibility without a full migration.

For small product-based businesses already using QuickBooks, it fills the gap between spreadsheets and a proper ERP. The limitation: once your business outgrows its boundaries, switching requires planning. Starting here can work, but treat it as a stepping stone.

**Strengths:** Tight QuickBooks integration, simple interface, 14-day trial.

**Weaknesses:** Limited to QuickBooks ecosystem, may not scale to complex inventory needs.

**Best for:** Existing QuickBooks users managing multi-channel retail or light wholesale who need inventory visibility without switching systems.

### 10. Katana ERP

Purpose-built for small manufacturers: make-to-order, D2C brands, workshops. Create a sales order and it automatically updates materials, schedules production, and adjusts inventory. No HR, no payroll, no unnecessary modules. Just the functions a small manufacturer actually needs.

The interface is visual and clean. Most users figure it out with minimal training. Integrates with Shopify, Xero, and other common small business tools.

**Strengths:** Focused on manufacturing workflows, easy to use, real-time inventory, $179 per month base.

**Weaknesses:** Not suitable for complex or enterprise-scale operations.

**Best for:** Small manufacturers (under 100 users) with make-to-order or D2C workflows who want a dedicated production management system.

## How SAP GROW changed the small-business ERP conversation in 2026

The four shifts that matter for SAP-curious small and mid-market buyers:

1. **GROW with SAP is the 2026 SAP entry point** for organisations 50 to 1,000 staff. Total programme cost runs $2M to $6M end-to-end, 6 to 9 months to go-live. That puts it in direct conversation with NetSuite and Dynamics 365 Business Central at the mid-market end, not against full enterprise S/4HANA on private edition (which runs $20M+ and is a different conversation entirely).
2. **Clean-core compliance is built into the GROW subscription.** Custom logic moves to SAP BTP side-by-side extensions. Standard configuration covers the digital core. The trade-off is real: less flexibility, lower upgrade risk, and a forced discipline that catches the legacy-process mistake before it lands.
3. **Joule for SAP Activate ships with GROW.** Fit-gap analyses, configuration documents, and test scripts draft 30 to 50 percent faster. The acceleration is real on standard configuration; complex industry logic still needs senior consultants.
4. **SAP Cloud ALM is the bundled application lifecycle backbone.** Requirements, deployment plans, change tracking, and operations monitoring all live in one cloud-native platform. No Solution Manager to stand up, no extra licence.

Where GROW wins on the shortlist: organisations that want a credible path to the broader SAP product family (Ariba for procurement, SuccessFactors for HR, Concur for travel, Analytics Cloud for reporting) as they scale. Where NetSuite or Business Central wins: organisations that need deep flexibility on day one, or that are not going to grow into enterprise-scale SAP.

Where GROW loses: heavily customised legacy environments that cannot live with the clean-core posture. Those organisations end up on S/4HANA private edition (RISE) at enterprise budget, not on GROW.

## How to choose

The wrong way to choose: pick the system a competitor uses, or buy based on features you might need in three years.

The right way: identify your biggest operational gap today, run a trial with your actual data, and involve the people who will use it daily, not just IT or procurement.

For most small businesses, the selection comes down to one of four profiles:

1. **Finance-led:** Sage Intacct
2. **Operations-heavy with growth plans:** Acumatica or NetSuite
3. **Microsoft ecosystem:** Dynamics 365 Business Central
4. **Budget-constrained:** Zoho One, ERPNext, or Odoo

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is an affordable ERP for small business?</summary>

Three options that genuinely keep costs down:

- **Odoo**: open-source with a free community version and low-cost hosted plans. Add only what you need.
- **ERPNext**: free to self-host. Broad coverage including manufacturing, accounting, and CRM. Requires technical capacity to maintain.
- **Zoho One**: $30/user/month for the full suite. The lowest cost option with meaningful out-of-the-box functionality.

Affordable has a ceiling. Once you need complex customisation, multi-entity accounting, or industry-specific logic, the cheaper options start requiring paid development to extend.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do small businesses need ERP?</summary>

Yes, when: you are managing operations across more than one system and spending hours reconciling data between them; your team is growing and onboarding takes weeks because workflows are not documented or consistent; inventory, sales, and finance are giving you different numbers for the same question.

ERP addresses operational coordination failures. It does not replace good processes, it makes consistent processes possible at scale.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the best ERP for a small manufacturer?</summary>

Katana for small make-to-order or D2C manufacturers who want a focused, easy-to-use system. Acumatica or Epicor Kinetic for manufacturers with more operational complexity who need deeper production scheduling, MES integration, or multi-warehouse inventory. SAP GROW if you want a path to full SAP capability as the business grows.

The test: can the system handle your bill of materials, production scheduling, and lot traceability requirements? If it cannot trace a defective part back to the original production run, it is not adequate for regulated manufacturing.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How much should a small business budget for ERP?</summary>

Plan the three-year total, not just the annual subscription. Include: implementation or setup costs (often 1–2x the first-year licence for a properly configured system), any customisation development, training, and annual support or maintenance fees.

Hidden cost pattern: add-on modules that are not included in the base price. NetSuite and SAP GROW both have modules that are functionally necessary but quoted separately. Ask vendors for a full three-year total cost scenario before signing.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What ERP can a very small business or solo founder use?</summary>

ERPNext and Zoho Books are the most accessible starting points. ERPNext handles invoicing, inventory, and CRM and can be used at no licence cost. Zoho Books plus Zoho Inventory covers basic operations for a few hundred dollars per year. QuickBooks Commerce is the right option if accounting is already on QuickBooks Online.

The consideration at this scale: avoid systems that require an implementation partner to configure. You want something that runs without external help until the business grows enough to justify the investment.

</details>
