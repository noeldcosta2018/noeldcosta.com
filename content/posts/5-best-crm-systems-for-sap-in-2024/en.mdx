For a company running SAP, the best CRM is usually the one whose integration with SAP survives real order volumes, messy master data and month-end close. In practice that means SAP Sales Cloud if you are starting fresh on S/4HANA, Salesforce if it is already embedded in your sales teams, and Dynamics 365 if you are a Microsoft shop. HubSpot and SugarAI fit mid-market cases.

This comparison is for CIOs and sales and finance leaders choosing a CRM to sit next to SAP. It covers the five options, list prices, the integration pattern each needs, and the mistakes that blow up CRM-SAP projects.

I have integrated Salesforce, Microsoft Dynamics 365, SAP Sales Cloud (both the legacy C4C-based product and V2), SAP Emarsys and HubSpot with SAP ERP back ends in manufacturing, retail and financial services. The same lesson keeps coming back: the CRM you choose matters less than the integration architecture around it.

I once spoke with a VP of Sales at a global manufacturing firm. They had SAP running the back end and a separate CRM for sales. The systems didn't sync. Orders were delayed, complaints piled up, forecasting was a mess, and sales made promises on stock that wasn't there. After the move to SAP Sales Cloud, reps could see real-time stock, support had order history, and customers got faster answers. The CRM choice mattered. The integration to S/4HANA mattered more.

## What breaks when CRM and SAP don't share data

1. **Sales promises stock that isn't there.** Pipeline assumes inventory that doesn't exist, and orders turn into backlog the moment customers ask for dates.
2. **Finance invoices orders that don't match.** Disputes, reissues, longer DSO. The CFO finds out at month-end.
3. **Support can't see order status.** Handle times grow and satisfaction drops, often before anyone links it to the CRM-SAP gap.

## The SAP customer experience portfolio

Before comparing third-party CRMs, know what SAP itself sells. SAP's customer experience (CX) products sit around S/4HANA:

1. **SAP Sales Cloud.** Sales force automation. Version 2, introduced in late 2022, is a rebuild of the older C4C-based product and is SAP's strategic direction. It integrates with S/4HANA for customers, products, pricing and order history using SAP-provided integration content.
2. **SAP Service Cloud.** Case management, knowledge base and field service, with S/4HANA integration for orders, returns, warranties and contracts.
3. **SAP Engagement Cloud.** The marketing automation product SAP acquired as Emarsys in 2020 and [renamed in February 2026](https://news.sap.com/2026/02/sap-engagement-cloud-advances-enterprise-engagement-strategy/). Strong in B2C, retail and e-commerce.
4. **SAP Commerce Cloud.** B2B and B2C commerce (originally Hybris), strong where catalogues and configurable products are complex.
5. **SAP Customer Data Platform.** Unifies customer identity and consent across the CX products and feeds segmentation.

Buyers sometimes skip SAP CX because they assume third-party CRMs are better. For an SAP shop, the integration economics usually favour SAP for core scenarios. The real question is where a third-party CRM adds enough value to justify the integration cost.

## The five CRM options compared

| CRM | Best for | SAP integration approach | Typical implementation | List price (per user/month, annual) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SAP Sales Cloud** | Companies already on SAP | SAP-provided integration content to S/4HANA | 3 to 6 months | Quote-based; check the SAP Store |
| **Salesforce Sales Cloud** | Large organisations, complex sales | MuleSoft, SAP Integration Suite or Boomi | 6 to 12 months | [$195 to $550](https://www.salesforce.com/sales/pricing/) (Core to Max editions) |
| **Microsoft Dynamics 365 Sales** | Microsoft-heavy environments | Power Automate, Azure Logic Apps, Integration Suite | 4 to 8 months | $65 to $150 |
| **HubSpot Sales Hub** | Mid-market, sales speed first | HubSpot data sync for simple flows; middleware for orders and stock | 2 to 5 months | $90 to $150, plus onboarding fees |
| **SugarAI (formerly SugarCRM) Sugar Sell** | Mid-market wanting configurability | Connector or middleware | 3 to 6 months | $68 to $155 |

List prices change often and enterprise deals are negotiated. Use these as a starting point, not a budget.

### SAP Sales Cloud: the lowest-risk path

The obvious choice for an organisation already running S/4HANA. Customer master, product catalogue, pricing and order history integrate using SAP's own content, and custom extensions can run on SAP BTP without touching the ERP core.

**What it does well:** pipeline management with S/4HANA stock visibility, service cases with full order history, a link to the Customer Data Platform, and Joule for activity prompts and pipeline analysis.

**Where it fits:** S/4HANA or ECC customers who want the lowest integration risk. If the alternative is a six-month project to connect a non-SAP CRM, this is the path of least resistance.

**Honest weakness:** V2's UI is far better than the old product but still less polished than Salesforce. Sales teams coming from Salesforce find it less intuitive in the first month. Plan the change management.

**Approximate total implementation cost:** $250K to $1.5M depending on scope and how many CX products are in play.

### Salesforce

Salesforce leads the CRM market for good reasons: deep sales process support, strong analytics, Agentforce for AI, and the AppExchange ecosystem. The long-standing challenge is connecting it to SAP, and there is no joint SAP-Salesforce integration product to make that go away.

**How it connects:** MuleSoft (owned by Salesforce since 2018) is the most common route, with an official [SAP S/4HANA connector](https://docs.mulesoft.com/sap-s4hana-cloud-connector/latest/) and published SAP accelerator templates. SAP Integration Suite is the SAP-side alternative. Boomi is the other credible iPaaS. Enterprise MuleSoft is a real budget line, often in the $100K to $200K a year range, not a connector fee.

**Where it fits:** organisations where Salesforce is already established, or large multi-country sales operations where its ecosystem matters more than the integration overhead.

**Honest weakness:** total cost of ownership. Licences, AI add-ons, middleware, implementation and support stack up, and the connector work is the line most often under-scoped.

**Approximate total implementation cost:** $400K to $2M+ for a full SAP-connected deployment.

### Microsoft Dynamics 365

The natural choice where Azure, Teams and Microsoft 365 are already in place. Power Automate is the stand-out integration advantage: one retail client connected S/4HANA inventory to Dynamics Sales in two days with Power Automate and no code.

At a manufacturing client in Germany I watched it work well: the sales team used Dynamics while operations ran on S/4HANA. The key was treating financial postings as a separate design exercise from the operational integration. SAP postings have to reconcile exactly with Dynamics orders, or month-end becomes a nightmare.

**Where it fits:** organisations standardised on Microsoft and running SAP alongside it.

**Honest weakness:** Copilot features demo well but need clean master data. Messy SAP master data makes the AI layer amplify noise.

**Approximate total implementation cost:** $200K to $1M.

### HubSpot

HubSpot has matured into a credible CRM for mid-market SAP environments. It wins on time-to-productivity and sales adoption. The SAP integration is less mature than Salesforce or Dynamics but workable for standard scenarios: contact and company sync natively, with middleware such as MuleSoft or Workato for stock, pricing and orders from S/4HANA.

**Where it fits:** mid-market organisations (roughly 50 to 500 users) where sales speed matters more than enterprise depth.

**Honest weakness:** not a substitute for Salesforce or Dynamics at enterprise scale. Its configuration model is opinionated; complex sales processes end up working around HubSpot's defaults.

**Approximate total implementation cost:** $100K to $400K including SAP integration.

### SugarAI (formerly SugarCRM)

More configurable than most mid-market options, for companies that want control without enterprise Salesforce pricing.

**Integration approach:** connectors cover customers, contacts, products and orders for standard SAP setups; the API handles custom flows such as opportunities creating SAP quotations. My advice is the same as for any CRM-SAP link: start small, connect one process first, get it working, then expand.

**Where it fits:** mid-market companies with in-house developers who can maintain custom integration.

**Approximate total implementation cost:** $100K to $400K.

## Integration patterns

The CRM is half the decision. The integration architecture is the other half.

<explainer-layers title="A CRM next to SAP, layer by layer" caption="The CRM is one layer. | Pattern | When it fits | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| SAP CX with SAP integration content | SAP Sales or Service Cloud on S/4HANA | Extensions outside the standard content still need design |
| SAP Integration Suite | SAP-centric landscapes connecting any CRM | Message-based pricing; needs SAP integration skills |
| MuleSoft | Salesforce-SAP at enterprise scale | Recurring licence cost; a separate skill set from SAP |
| Boomi | Mid-market iPaaS for mixed landscapes | Fewer SAP-specific templates |
| Power Automate / Azure Logic Apps | Dynamics-SAP in Microsoft estates | Less suited to high-volume real-time flows |

### Where AI on CRM-SAP actually helps

Joule on SAP Sales Cloud, Agentforce on Salesforce and Copilot on Dynamics promise similar things. The differences in value come mostly from data quality.

AI helps with deal scoring when pipeline data is clean, next-best-action prompts when case history is consistent, and segmentation when customer identity is reliable. It doesn't help yet with forecasting on poorly kept pipelines, activity summaries when capture is patchy, or pricing optimisation when pricing data is fragmented.

AI on top of CRM-SAP is real for clean-data organisations and theatre for everyone else. Invest in master data first.

## Integration mistakes that kill projects

**Underestimating complexity.** Vendors downplay the difficulty. The "simple connector" one customer bought needed six months of custom development to work. Ask about custom fields, approval workflows, multi-entity support and clean core rules before signing.

**Skipping the performance test.** A manufacturing client skipped testing with real workloads, and its month-end close went from 4 hours to 3 days after the CRM integration went live. Test with real transaction volumes before cutover. The problem only appears at scale.

**No single source of truth.** SAP and the CRM will disagree about which record is right. Decide per data domain before go-live: customer master usually wins in SAP, opportunity ownership in the CRM, pricing in SAP. Write it down.

**No support model.** When the integration breaks at 2 AM, someone has to understand both systems. Most organisations discover the gap on the first weekend incident.

**No continuity plan.** A retail client did its cutover without a backup plan and couldn't process orders for three days. Cutover continuity is its own work package. ## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>Should I use SAP's own CRM or a third-party option?</summary>

If you are on S/4HANA and starting fresh, SAP Sales Cloud is the lowest-risk choice: SAP-provided integration content and one vendor for support. Third-party CRMs, especially Salesforce, often have more polished interfaces and bigger ecosystems.

The existing landscape usually decides it. Microsoft shop with no CRM: consider Dynamics. Salesforce already in part of the business: connect it rather than rip it out. Starting fresh on SAP: SAP Sales Cloud first.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is there an official SAP and Salesforce integration?</summary>

Not a joint product. Salesforce's MuleSoft provides an SAP S/4HANA connector and SAP accelerator templates, and SAP Integration Suite can connect to Salesforce on the SAP side. Partners also sell prebuilt integration content. Treat any "out of the box" claim as a starting point and test it with your own data.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why do CRM-SAP integrations fail?</summary>

Unclear scope of what syncs and in which direction, performance problems at real volumes, master data problems found after go-live, no agreed system of record, and complexity underestimated at scoping. The most reliable predictor of failure is a rushed design phase.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How long does a CRM-SAP integration take?</summary>

SAP Sales Cloud: 3 to 6 months for a standard deployment. Salesforce or Dynamics with complex integration: 6 to 12 months. HubSpot or SugarAI with limited scope: 2 to 5 months. Messy master data, heavy customisation or part-time staff can double these.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What middleware works best for connecting a third-party CRM to SAP?</summary>

For Salesforce-SAP at enterprise scale, MuleSoft is the most common choice, with Boomi as the alternative. For Dynamics-SAP in Microsoft estates, Power Automate and Azure Logic Apps are usually enough. SAP Integration Suite is the natural default when you want SAP-aligned tooling and support. For simple HubSpot or SugarAI flows, lighter tools such as Workato can work; Zapier does not scale to enterprise volumes.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can Salesforce replace SAP for CRM?</summary>

Salesforce covers the customer-facing side well: sales, service, marketing and partners. It does not replace SAP's finance, supply chain, procurement or manufacturing. Most organisations with both connect them. The real question is how to make them work together without creating a maintenance burden. Judge the options on what they cost to keep running in year three, not year one.

</details>
