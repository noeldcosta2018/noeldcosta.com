SAP ECC mainstream maintenance ends on 31 December 2027, about 15 months from now. Optional extended maintenance runs to the end of 2030 at a higher fee ([SAP News](https://news.sap.com/2020/02/sap-s4hana-maintenance-2040-clarity-choice-sap-business-suite-7/)). Complex migrations can easily stretch 18 to 24 months once real testing starts. If you have not chosen a path yet, a go-live before the deadline is already unlikely.

If you are the CIO or programme lead making that call, you have three paths: greenfield, brownfield or selective data transition. The work behind each follows the same five steps. Run the SAP Readiness Check first, then choose.

ECC to S/4HANA is not a straight upgrade. It changes how data is stored, how transactions post and how users work. In one project I worked on, a team had dozens of custom reports rebuilt exactly as they were in ECC. Nobody asked whether they were still needed. Later, half of them were never used. The migration was technically clean. The value was not.

## What actually changes between ECC and S/4HANA

The differences that drive migration effort:

| Area | SAP ECC | SAP S/4HANA |
|---|---|---|
| Database | Any supported database (Oracle, Db2, SQL Server, others) | SAP HANA only |
| Finance data model | Separate FI, CO and profitability tables with totals tables | Universal Journal (table ACDOCA) as the single line-item source |
| Customer and vendor master | Separate customer and vendor records | Business partner is mandatory |
| User interface | SAP GUI | SAP Fiori apps, with SAP GUI still available on-premise and Private Edition |
| Deployment | On-premise | On-premise, Private Edition (RISE with SAP) or Public Edition (GROW with SAP) |
| Extensions | Z-programs and modifications | Clean core: released APIs, on-stack ABAP Cloud or side-by-side on SAP BTP |
| Maintenance | EHP 6 to 8 mainstream to end 2027, extended to end 2030 | SAP committed to maintenance until the end of 2040 |

One client I worked with kept asking why their custom batch jobs were no longer working after conversion. The logic relied on ECC table structures that S/4HANA had changed. The migration itself was complete. Nobody had asked whether those jobs still made sense in the new model.

The migration moves the data. The real question is what you do with the design decisions you inherit.

## Three migration paths

<decision-tree question="Which migration path fits your situation?" options="Legacy is fragmented and you want to redesign processes => Greenfield|Processes are sound, ECC is stable, history must stay => Brownfield|Multi-entity, want partial reuse and selective data => Bluefield"></decision-tree>

### Greenfield: start fresh

A new implementation of S/4HANA. No legacy configuration carried forward, processes redesigned against SAP's standard, and only the data you need loaded with the SAP S/4HANA Migration Cockpit.

**Use when** legacy systems are too fragmented to convert cleanly, or the business wants to rethink processes rather than copy them.

**Watch for** the change load. Users lose familiar workflows. I have seen companies regret not preparing their users for how different things feel in a greenfield system. If adoption planning starts at training, it is already late.

### Brownfield: system conversion

Your existing ECC system is converted in place. Configuration, custom code and history come with you. The technical conversion runs through the Software Update Manager (SUM) with its database migration option.

**Use when** processes are mature, transaction history matters for compliance and no major restructuring is under way.

**Watch for** the technical debt you carry over. Legacy complexity moves with you unless you clean it up deliberately during the conversion.

### Selective data transition

Sometimes sold as "bluefield". You move chosen company codes, business units or time slices of data, not everything, usually with specialist tooling and a partner experienced in it. It suits groups built through mergers or carve-outs, or where years of history no longer matter.

**Use when** you want greenfield-style process freedom with brownfield-style data continuity where it counts.

This is how the three compare on the questions boards usually ask:

| Criteria | Greenfield | Brownfield | Selective |
|---|---|---|---|
| Process redesign | Full, against SAP standard | Largely preserved | Chosen per unit |
| Historical data | Not carried forward (or only open items and balances) | Fully carried forward | Selected scope |
| Time to go-live | Longer | Shorter | Depends on scope |
| Technical debt | Removed | Carried forward | Reduced for migrated scope |
| Change impact | High | Lower | Moderate |
| Best for | Fragmented legacy, major redesign | Stable, well-kept ECC | Mergers, carve-outs, phased rollouts |

## Five steps that make up the work

<stepper title="The migration sequence" steps="Assess|Classify data|Cleanse|Adapt code|Test and cut over" bodies="Run the SAP Readiness Check. Inventory custom code, add-ons and interfaces.|Segment data as hot, warm and cold. Cold data does not have to move.|Resolve duplicates, inconsistencies and missing fields. Always longer than planned.|Run the ABAP Test Cockpit checks. Reduce Z-code, do not just port it.|Several test rounds plus at least one full cutover rehearsal."></stepper>

### 1. Assessment and readiness

Start here. Always. The SAP Readiness Check analyses your ECC system and reports on simplification items, add-on compatibility, custom code, data volumes and sizing.

The surprises are usually the add-ons and the custom code. Some clients I have worked with had hundreds of custom objects in scope, and most are surprised by how much inactive code shows up. Better to find that in week two than in month twelve.

Check the technical prerequisites at the same time. Conversion works from SAP ERP 6.0 on any enhancement package. The system must be Unicode, or you plan a two-step conversion. Dual-stack systems need a split first. Customer and vendor masters must be converted to business partners before the conversion. That last one catches more teams than any other.

### 2. Data analysis and classification

Measure your data before you move it, and sort it:

1. **Hot:** used often, needed for live processing.
2. **Warm:** occasional access, relevant but not daily.
3. **Cold:** historical or unused, kept for audit only.

Cold data does not need to go into S/4HANA. Archive it. Teams that skip this step bring clutter into the new system that slows both migration and performance.

### 3. Data cleansing and archiving

This step takes longer than every plan allows. Duplicate vendor records. Inconsistent units of measure. Half-filled customer masters. These problems exist in every ECC system, and they move into S/4HANA intact if nobody fixes them first.

One client I worked with spent five months just on data cleansing and archiving. Teams that skip cleansing find the problems during cutover, when there is no time to fix them properly. My article on [why SAP data migration fails](/why-sap-data-migration-fails-and-how-to-fix-it/) goes deeper on this step.

### 4. Custom code adaptation

Run the ABAP Test Cockpit (ATC) S/4HANA readiness checks, which compare your code against SAP's simplification items. The output shows what needs a syntax fix, what needs functional replacement and what should be retired.

The goal is less Z-code, not just working Z-code. Every custom object you carry over adds cost to every future upgrade. My [clean core guide](/sap-clean-core-strategy-what-it-means-for-your-business/) covers how to classify what remains.

### 5. Testing, training and cutover

Run testing in rounds: unit, integration, user acceptance testing (UAT), then at least one full cutover rehearsal. Put both power users and occasional users in UAT. They find different problems.

The rehearsal is not optional. It exposes timing gaps, missing validations and interface failures that only appear when the whole sequence runs. Teams that skip it meet those problems on go-live weekend.

## Tools that help

The SAP tools I would expect to see in any migration plan:

| Tool | What it does | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| SAP Readiness Check | Simplification items, add-ons, custom code, sizing | Before choosing the path |
| ABAP Test Cockpit (ATC) | Finds custom code that will break | Custom code adaptation |
| SAP Signavio | Shows how processes actually run, not how they were documented | Before design freeze |
| SAP LeanIX | Maps applications and interfaces | Integration design |
| Software Update Manager (SUM) | Runs the technical conversion | Brownfield conversion |
| SAP S/4HANA Migration Cockpit | Loads master and transactional data | Greenfield data migration |

## Timelines and the 2027 deadline

For SAP ERP 6.0 on enhancement packages 6 to 8, mainstream maintenance ends on 31 December 2027. Optional extended maintenance runs to 31 December 2030 at a premium of two percentage points on the maintenance base. Earlier enhancement packages left mainstream maintenance at the end of 2025.

Beyond 2030 there is one narrow route. SAP's [SAP ERP, private edition, transition option](https://news.sap.com/2025/08/rise-with-sap-journey-sap-erp-private-edition-transition-option-updates/) covers 2031 to 2033. It applies only to large systems moved to SAP ERP, private edition on SAP HANA before the end of 2030, and only with SAP's max success plan. Treat it as an exception, not a plan.

On duration, full migrations of complex systems can stretch 18 to 24 months or more once real testing starts. For mid-size to large companies, 12 to 18 months or more is realistic for a well-prepared conversion. A large greenfield programme across several entities can run 24 to 36 months.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between greenfield and brownfield ECC to S/4HANA migration?</summary>

Greenfield is a new implementation: no legacy configuration or custom code is carried forward, processes are designed against SAP's standard and only the data you need is loaded. Brownfield converts your existing ECC system, keeping configuration, custom code and history. It is faster and less disruptive, but technical debt comes with it. Selective data transition sits between the two.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the SAP ECC to S/4HANA migration deadline?</summary>

For SAP ERP 6.0 on enhancement packages 6 to 8, mainstream maintenance ends on 31 December 2027. Optional extended maintenance runs to 31 December 2030 at a premium of two percentage points on the maintenance base. A transition option for 2031 to 2033 exists only for large systems moved to SAP ERP, private edition on SAP HANA before the end of 2030.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What does the SAP Readiness Check tell you?</summary>

It analyses your ECC system and reports on simplification items that affect your configuration, add-on compatibility, custom code impact, data volumes and HANA sizing. Running it early changes the scope discussion, because teams regularly find add-ons and custom code they did not know they depended on.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How long does an ECC to S/4HANA migration take?</summary>

A well-prepared conversion for a mid-size to large company often takes 12 to 18 months or more. Complex, multi-entity programmes can stretch 18 to 24 months once real testing starts, and large greenfield programmes 24 to 36 months. Data cleansing is the most common reason schedules slip.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the Universal Journal (ACDOCA) and why does it matter for migration?</summary>

The Universal Journal is the single line-item table, ACDOCA, that brings together financial accounting, controlling and profitability data that ECC kept in separate tables. Custom code and reports that read the old structures, such as COEP or the profitability tables, need review. Some need small adjustments; others need redesign.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the technical prerequisites for converting ECC to S/4HANA?</summary>

Conversion works from SAP ERP 6.0 on any enhancement package. The system must be Unicode, or you use a two-step approach. Dual-stack systems must be split first. Customer and vendor masters must be converted to business partners. Run the SAP Readiness Check and the ATC custom code checks before you commit to dates.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Should I choose RISE with SAP or GROW with SAP for an ECC migration?</summary>

Most ECC customers with significant custom code and complex processes go to S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition under RISE with SAP, which supports conversion of the existing system. GROW with SAP uses Public Edition, which is a new implementation with standard processes and released-API extensions only. It suits companies willing to adopt SAP's standard.

</details>
