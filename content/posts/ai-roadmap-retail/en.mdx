A retail AI roadmap has four stages: one trusted view of product, stock and customer, a governed intelligence layer on top, AI agents for forecasting, replenishment, pricing and service, then AI embedded across merchandising, supply chain and stores. Retailers who start with a personalisation engine usually find the product and stock data underneath it isn't good enough.

Retail produces more data than almost any industry: every basket, every return, every click and every delivery. It also trusts its data less than most. Stores, e-commerce, the warehouse and finance often report different stock and different sales for the same day.

This roadmap is technology neutral. It works whatever your ERP, point of sale, order management and e-commerce platforms are. ## Now: build the data foundation

A retailer's data foundation has to join systems that grew up separately: the ERP, point of sale, e-commerce, order management, the warehouse, loyalty and the supplier portal.

1. **A modern data platform.** A lakehouse that takes sales, stock, orders, returns and loyalty data from every channel, close to real time where it matters.
2. **Governance, catalogue and lineage.** Product, location and customer data with owners and quality rules. Card data stays out of scope and inside your PCI DSS controls.
3. **Self-service reporting.** Buyers, planners and store managers see the same sales and stock figures as finance.

The product master is usually the hardest part.

Sizes, colours, packs, suppliers and attributes held differently online and in stores. Clean it here. Forecasting, pricing and search all depend on it.

## Year 1: a governed intelligence layer

The core of year one is a semantic layer: one business language for product, category, store, channel, stock position, promotion and customer. An agent asked about "availability in the top 50 stores" must calculate it the same way as the trading director.

AI governance comes in the same year. Personalisation and pricing use customer data, so consent and data protection rules decide what is allowed. Keep one register of AI use cases with owners, risk ratings, testing and human review, and be clear about which decisions customers can see.

Then deliver quick wins.

1. **Product content.** Descriptions, attributes and translations generated from the product master, checked by a person.
2. **Store copilots.** Store teams ask about stock, deliveries, promotions and procedures in plain language.
3. **Customer service summaries.** Contact history and order status summarised so agents resolve issues faster.

## Year 2: scale AI agents across the business

Year two moves from assistants to agents that act within limits. In retail, these pay back first.

1. **Forecasting and replenishment.** The agent forecasts by store and day, proposes orders and rebalances stock between stores before shelves go empty.
2. **Pricing and markdowns.** It proposes markdowns for slow sellers and price changes within the rules the commercial team sets.
3. **Store operations.** Shelf cameras or RFID flag gaps and misplaced stock, and the agent creates the task for the store team.
4. **Customer service.** It handles order tracking, returns and simple complaints, and hands complex cases to a person with the context ready.

Each agent needs an owner in the business. A pricing agent belongs to the commercial director, not to IT.

## Year 3: AI embedded at scale

By year three AI sits inside merchandising, supply chain, stores and customer engagement. Buyers, planners and store teams work with agents every day, and the whole chain is governed end to end.

Data sharing with suppliers changes the economics.

Shared sell-through and forecasts mean suppliers plan production against real demand. Retail media and partner channels run on the same governed customer layer, with consent. The result is a differentiated customer experience and a cost base competitors find hard to match.

## Across every stage

1. **Customer consent and privacy.** Personalisation only works if customers trust it. Use data they agreed to share, and keep card data inside PCI DSS controls.
2. **An open, multi-platform ecosystem.** Retail technology changes fast. Keep the freedom to swap models, platforms and partners.
3. **Governance that matches the risk.** Light controls for content and copilots, stronger ones for pricing and anything customers see.
4. **Your people upskilled.** Store teams and buyers decide whether the agents get used. Train them early and listen to them.

## Where retail AI roadmaps go wrong

The most common mistake is buying a personalisation or pricing engine before the product and stock data can support it. The engine recommends products that are out of stock, or prices against the wrong cost. Trust goes in weeks and takes a year to rebuild.

The second is running AI as a head-office project. Stores see agents as more work unless the tasks they create are right. Pilot with real store managers and fix what they tell you before scaling.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is a retail AI roadmap?</summary>

It is a staged plan for using AI across a retail business. It runs from one trusted view of product, stock and customer, through a governed intelligence layer and AI agents, to AI embedded in merchandising, supply chain and stores.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Where should a retailer start with AI?</summary>

Start with product, stock and sales data from every channel in one governed platform, and fix the product master. Then deliver quick wins such as product content, store copilots and customer service summaries.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Which retail AI use cases pay back first?</summary>

Product content and service summaries are quick. Demand forecasting and replenishment usually deliver the most value once the data is ready, followed by pricing and markdown optimisation.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do retailers need a new ERP before using AI?</summary>

No. AI sits on top of your current systems through the data foundation. What matters is that product, stock and sales data is consistent across stores, online and finance.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How long does a retail AI roadmap take?</summary>

I plan it over three years once the data foundation is under way: a governed intelligence layer in year one, agents in year two and AI embedded across the business in year three. Quick wins should show value in the first year.

</details>
