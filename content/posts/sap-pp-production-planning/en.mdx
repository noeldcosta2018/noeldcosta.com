SAP PP (Production Planning) is the part of SAP S/4HANA that turns demand into a production plan. It takes forecasts and sales orders, checks them against stock, bills of materials, routings and capacity, and proposes what to make, what to buy and when. This guide is for plant managers, planners and project leads who need to know what PP does, what changed in S/4HANA, and where implementations break. The short answer on the last point is master data. If the material master, BOMs, routings and work centres are wrong, MRP output is wrong, and no configuration fixes that. The readiness checklist further down is where to start.

I once worked with a team at a packaging plant that had over 400 production orders a week. Without PP, they were juggling spreadsheets and sticky notes. After implementation, they did not become perfect, but they stopped drowning.

## What SAP PP manages

PP sits at the centre of the logistics chain. It takes demand and works backwards through materials, capacity and timing to generate production proposals. The functions, in the order demand flows through them:

1. **Sales and Operations Planning (SOP)**: high-level volumes based on forecasts and market expectations
2. **Demand management**: converts those volumes into Planned Independent Requirements (PIRs) that drive MRP
3. **Material Requirements Planning (MRP)**: works out what to produce or buy, when, and in what quantity, using stock, lead times and open orders
4. **Capacity planning and levelling**: shows work centre overloads before they reach the shop floor
5. **Production order management**: creates, releases, schedules and confirms orders through to completion

PP covers discrete manufacturing (car parts, electronics, industrial goods) and repetitive manufacturing (packaging, consumer goods). Batch and recipe-based production in food, chemicals and pharmaceuticals uses PP-PI (Production Planning for Process Industries). PP-PI works with process orders and master recipes instead of production orders and routings.

## How the planning cycle runs

The flow from demand to completion has five stages. In practice it rarely runs without interruption, but this is the structure PP is built to manage.

<explainer-flow title="How demand becomes production in SAP PP" caption="Each stage feeds the next, so a mistake in the PIRs or the master data shows up in every stage after it." steps="SOP|Demand management|MRP|Production execution|Confirmation" notes="Volume targets from forecasts|PIRs that drive the MRP run|Planned orders and purchase requisitions|Orders released, materials staged|Labour, quantities and goods movements booked" result="SAP reflects what actually happened on the floor"></explainer-flow>

**SOP** is where high-level production targets are set. Some businesses run this rigorously against market data. Others use rough estimates. Either way, it feeds demand management. In S/4HANA the classic SOP transactions are compatibility scope, which I cover below.

**Demand management** generates PIRs. A PIR is a forecast: we expect to need this quantity of this product at this time. PIRs are the input to MRP, and mistakes here cascade. A client once uploaded duplicate PIRs covering three months. No one noticed until production started overordering everything.

**MRP** is the planning engine. It nets PIRs and sales orders against stock and open receipts, then creates planned orders for in-house production and purchase requisitions for bought-in parts. A well-configured run brings clarity. A run built on inaccurate master data produces noise that planners spend weeks chasing.

**Production execution** is where planned orders become production orders. Orders are released, materials are staged, and operations run at the work centres.

**Confirmation** closes the loop. Labour is booked, quantities recorded and goods movements posted. The system then reflects what actually happened on the floor, provided people confirm on time.

## Master data: where most implementations break

Master data is the backbone of SAP PP. Not as a saying. As a fact with consequences.

**Material master (MRP views).** Every manufactured material needs its MRP views set correctly. MRP type, lot size, strategy group, availability check and scheduling margin key each change what MRP does. One missing field in a material master blocked production for a week on a project I was on. One field.

**Bills of materials (BOM).** The BOM defines what goes into a finished product: components, quantities, scrap percentages, sometimes alternatives. BOMs can have variants, which suits configurable products and confuses teams new to SAP. The question "do we really need five BOMs for this one item?" has a real answer. Work it out before implementation, not during MRP runs.

**Routings.** A routing defines how something is made: the sequence of operations, which work centres perform them, and how long each takes. Without one, SAP cannot calculate production duration or check capacity. Even simple products need clear routings, and rushed projects skip them.

**Work centres.** Work centres are where production happens: machines, lines, groups of people. They carry scheduling formulas, costing links and shift calendars. Keeping them current after go-live is the task that gets dropped. When it is, capacity planning goes inaccurate and the planners stop trusting the system.

**Production versions.** A production version ties a valid BOM to a valid routing for a date range and lot size. In S/4HANA they are mandatory for in-house produced materials, and MRP Live will not explode a BOM without one. ECC customers who relied on BOM selection methods need to create them during conversion.

### Organisational structure

These are the structure elements PP depends on and where each one links.

| Structure element | Purpose in SAP PP | Key linkage |
|---|---|---|
| Client | Top-level enterprise structure | Common across all modules |
| Company code | Legal entity for financial reporting | FI cost tracking of production |
| Plant | Core unit where production occurs | Links PP with MM, SD, PM |
| Storage location | Physical inventory subdivision within plant | Material staging and movement |
| MRP area | Planning scope for separate MRP runs | Replaces storage location MRP in S/4HANA |
| Work centre | Production resource (machine, line, group) | Routing, capacity planning, CO cost centres |
| Production version | Valid BOM and routing combination for a material | Mandatory for MRP and order creation in S/4HANA |

### Readiness checklist before the first MRP run

Run this sequence before you let MRP loose on migrated data. The owner in brackets is who signs it off.

1. **Material master MRP views complete** for every planned material, including strategy group and lot size (planning lead)
2. **BOMs validated against what is actually built**, with engineering confirming components and scrap (engineering)
3. **Routings exist for every in-house material**, with realistic operation times (production engineering)
4. **Work centre calendars and capacities reflect current shifts** (plant manager)
5. **One valid production version per in-house material**, covering the planning horizon (planning lead)
6. **PIRs loaded once**, reconciled to the approved demand plan before the first run (demand planner)
7. **Open orders and stock reconciled** between legacy and SAP (inventory controller)
8. **A test MRP run on a single plant or MRP area**, with planners reviewing exceptions before the full run (planning lead)

## Integration points that decide success

PP does not work alone. Each integration point is also a failure point.

**MM (Materials Management).** PP generates material requirements and MM sources them. If availability is wrong in MM, production orders hit shortages. A good PP and MM integration is what ends emergency procurement runs.

**SD (Sales and Distribution).** Sales orders can consume PIRs in demand management. The strategy group in the material master controls how. If it is wrong, sales orders and forecasts run in parallel instead of netting off, doubling the demand signal with no error message. My [SAP SD guide](/sap-sd-sales-and-distribution/) covers the order side of that link.

**CO (Controlling).** Production orders carry costs. CO tracks actual labour, material and overhead against standard cost, and settlement posts the variances. Without it, the financial picture of production is incomplete.

**QM (Quality Management).** PP events can trigger QM inspection lots, and quality results can hold up orders. This is what makes quality a process control rather than an afterthought.

## What changed in S/4HANA

If you are moving from ECC, these are the PP changes that affect design and data. The details sit in SAP's simplification list and the compatibility scope matrix (SAP Note 2269324).

1. **MRP Live (transaction MD01N)** is SAP's recommended planning run. It runs inside the HANA database, can plan several plants in one run, and does not write MRP lists. Classic MD01 to MD03 still exist. SAP's [MRP Live learning module](https://learning.sap.com/courses/exploring-production-planning-in-sap-s-4hana/outlining-material-requirements-planning-with-mrp-live-1) is a good primer.
2. **Production versions are mandatory** for in-house production, as above.
3. **MRP areas replace storage location MRP.** Storage locations planned separately in ECC need an MRP area in S/4HANA.
4. **SOP, MRP lists and forecast-based planning (MRP type VV) are compatibility scope.** Usage rights ended on 31 December 2025 for on-premise systems. SAP extended them to 31 December 2030 for S/4HANA systems under a RISE with SAP or SAP Cloud ERP Private contract. SAP's named successor for SOP is [SAP IBP](/why-sap-integrated-business-planning-sap-ibp-matters/).
5. **Embedded PP/DS** (Production Planning and Detailed Scheduling) runs inside S/4HANA for finite scheduling. MRP Live hands materials flagged for advanced planning to PP/DS heuristics. Check your licence before you design around it.

The [SAP Help page on working with MRP Live](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_S4HANA_ON-PREMISE/f899ce30af9044299d573ea30b533f1c/86e15c58eb021f60e10000000a44147b.html) has the configuration detail.

## Failure modes and risk

I have seen some real disasters over the years, and these are the patterns to avoid. Under RISE with SAP, the clean-core approach limits the custom code that used to hide these problems. Bad master data now shows up faster, and costs more to work around.

**Rushing master data migration.** I remember one automotive parts company that rushed its BOM and routing migration. It spent six months after go-live fixing production orders that kept failing on data problems. Master data validation before migration is not a side task. It is the project. My piece on [why SAP data migration fails](/why-sap-data-migration-fails-and-how-to-fix-it/) goes further.

**User resistance.** Experienced planners often resist PP because it takes over work they used to do by hand and judgement. Bring them into configuration early. Show them where PP gives them time back. That decides adoption.

**Replicating legacy processes.** The most consistent configuration mistake I see: setting SAP up to work exactly like the old process instead of adopting standard planning logic. The result is an over-configured system that delivers less than PP was designed to.

**Undertesting integrations.** Every connection to MM, SD, CO and QM can fail. Teams that test each module alone discover integration problems in production.

**Cutting training.** I have seen companies spend millions on implementation and then save money by cutting training. Users who do not understand the system create workarounds. Workarounds break the data accuracy MRP depends on.

The table maps each risk to its impact and the mitigation I would put in the plan.

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Incorrect MRP configuration | Stockouts, overproduction, excess procurement cost | Validate MRP types, strategy groups and lot sizing with business leads |
| Inaccurate BOM or routing | Wrong material requirements, shop floor delays | Involve engineering in BOM and routing reviews before migration |
| Poor capacity planning | Work centre bottlenecks, missed delivery dates | Run capacity evaluation and levelling before releasing orders |
| Missing production version | MRP cannot explode the BOM or create valid orders | One valid production version per in-house material |
| No real-time shop floor feedback | Inaccurate production status, late visibility of deviations | Enforce timely confirmations; integrate SAP Digital Manufacturing or another MES |
| Undertrained users | Wrong confirmations, backflush errors, unresolved rework | Role-specific training on real production cycles |
| Variant configuration not handled | Wrong orders for configurable products | Test configuration logic across BOM and routing together |

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is SAP PP and what does it do?</summary>

SAP PP (Production Planning) is the SAP module for manufacturing planning and execution. It manages bills of materials, work centres, routings and production orders. Its core job is connecting demand from forecasts and sales orders to production capacity through MRP, then running the production order from creation to settlement.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What master data does SAP PP require?</summary>

Five objects: the material master MRP views, bills of materials, routings, work centres and, in S/4HANA, production versions. Each has to be accurate at go-live and maintained after it. A single missing field in the material master can stop production orders being created.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How does MRP work in SAP S/4HANA?</summary>

MRP nets demand (PIRs, sales orders, dependent requirements) against stock and open receipts, using lead times, lot sizes and safety stock from the material master. It outputs planned orders for in-house production and purchase requisitions for external procurement. In S/4HANA, SAP recommends MRP Live (MD01N), which runs in the HANA database and does not create MRP lists.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is a production order in SAP PP?</summary>

A production order is the instruction to make a specific quantity of a material by a specific date. It is usually converted from an MRP planned order. Its lifecycle runs from creation and release to confirmation, goods receipt and settlement, where actual costs are compared with standard and the variance posts to CO.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between SAP PP and SAP PP-PI?</summary>

SAP PP is built for discrete and repetitive manufacturing, using production orders based on BOMs and routings. SAP PP-PI is built for batch and recipe-based production in food, beverages, chemicals and pharmaceuticals. It uses process orders and master recipes, and adds co-products, by-products and batch traceability. For regulated industries that batch-level trail is a compliance requirement.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is SOP still available in SAP S/4HANA?</summary>

Classic SOP is in the S/4HANA compatibility scope, with limited usage rights. Those rights ended on 31 December 2025 for on-premise systems and run to 31 December 2030 for systems under a RISE with SAP or SAP Cloud ERP Private contract. SAP's recommended successor is SAP Integrated Business Planning (IBP).

</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the most common SAP PP implementation mistakes?</summary>

Rushing master data, setting the wrong strategy group, copying legacy processes into configuration, skipping full integration testing and cutting training. Most of them end the same way: MRP output nobody trusts, and planners back in spreadsheets.

</details>
