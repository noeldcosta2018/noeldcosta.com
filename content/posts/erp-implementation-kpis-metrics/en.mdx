The ERP implementation KPIs that matter are a small set reviewed weekly during delivery and daily in hypercare, each with a named owner: schedule adherence, cost variance, scope change, test pass rate, data migration accuracy and, above all, user adoption. Below are the 30 I use, split into delivery and post-go-live, with formulas and the thresholds that should trigger action.

This is for programme directors, PMO leads and sponsors who need a steering pack that catches problems in week 8, not month 18.

A client once added 73 "small" changes to an SAP project. None looked significant on its own. Together they caused a five-month delay. Nobody had been tracking scope change volume. (If that sounds familiar, my guide to [avoiding scope creep in SAP projects](/how-to-avoid-scope-creep-in-an-sap-implementation/) covers the controls.)

Another client ignored early schedule warnings, and a one-year project took eighteen months. A retail client blew past early budget warnings and ended up cutting key features just to finish.

These are not unusual failures. They are what happens when teams track the wrong things, or nothing at all.

## During the project (KPIs 1 to 15)

| # | KPI | What it measures | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Schedule adherence** | Actual vs planned task completion | First signal of cascading delays |
| 2 | **Cost variance** | Actual spend vs budget by phase | Catches overruns before they compound |
| 3 | **Scope change volume** | Number and impact of approved changes | Uncontrolled change is the most common cause of overruns |
| 4 | **Resource utilisation** | Hours worked vs planned; workload balance | Overloaded people burn out or leave mid-project |
| 5 | **User adoption rate** | Share of target users actively using the system | The only metric that tells you the system is working for the business |
| 6 | **Training effectiveness** | Assessment scores; share of users trained | Predicts adoption failure before go-live |
| 7 | **Data migration accuracy** | Share of records migrated clean; error rate | Bad data in a new system takes months to clean |
| 8 | **Test environment downtime** | Hours of unplanned downtime in test systems | Instability in test predicts instability at go-live |
| 9 | **Engagement score** | Survey results; attendance at key sessions | Early warning of resistance before it surfaces publicly |
| 10 | **Risk resolution rate** | Share of open risks closed on schedule | Measure closure, not just identification |
| 11 | **Partner performance** | Deliverable quality; milestone hit rate | Partners that miss early deliverables almost always miss later ones |
| 12 | **Testing pass rate** | Share of test cases passing first time | Below 85% in SIT usually means systemic issues, not random bugs |
| 13 | **Change request turnaround** | Days from request to decision | Long queues signal a governance failure |
| 14 | **Budget burn rate** | Spend vs total budget, against work completed | Shows whether money and progress move together |
| 15 | **Configuration progress** | Share of planned configuration items done | Slippage here pushes testing and training downstream |

## Post-go-live (KPIs 16 to 30)

| # | KPI | What it measures | Threshold or note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | **System availability** | Uptime after go-live | Over 99.9% is good; under 99% becomes a user confidence problem |
| 17 | **Report and dashboard speed** | Load times; refresh rates | If managers export to Excel, the system is not delivering |
| 18 | **Employee productivity** | Task time vs pre-go-live baseline | A distribution client that automated approvals processed 25% more transactions a day after go-live |
| 19 | **First contact resolution** | Tickets resolved on first contact | Measures hypercare effectiveness |
| 20 | **Support ticket volume** | Open tickets; average resolution time | A spike around day 30 usually signals training gaps, not system bugs |
| 21 | **Process cycle times** | Order processing, invoice approval, close cycle | The outcome executives actually care about |
| 22 | **Inventory accuracy** | Physical vs system counts | The most visible data quality indicator after go-live |
| 23 | **Order fulfilment rate** | Orders fulfilled on time in the new system | Direct operational impact |
| 24 | **Revenue attribution** | Revenue changes linked to new capabilities | Long-term proof for the business case |
| 25 | **Compliance adherence** | Audit findings; regulatory issues | Matters most in finance, pharma and regulated sectors |
| 26 | **Forecast accuracy** | Forecast vs actual demand | Shows whether planning is being used and trusted |
| 27 | **User satisfaction** | Usability survey; key-user NPS | Users who hate the system build workarounds |
| 28 | **Process efficiency** | Time and cost per process vs baseline | Justifies the investment to the board |
| 29 | **Savings delivered** | Actual savings vs business case | The CFO will ask at 6 and 12 months |
| 30 | **Return on investment** | Net benefits divided by total cost | Usually measured at 12 and 24 months |

## Five KPIs with formulas

These are the ones people ask about most.

1. **Schedule performance index (SPI)** = earned value ÷ planned value. Above 1.0 is ahead of schedule, 1.0 on time, below 1.0 delayed.
2. **Cost performance index (CPI)** = earned value ÷ actual cost. Above 1.0 is efficient, below 1.0 over budget.
3. **Scope change percentage** = (approved changes ÷ initial scope items) × 100. Under 10% is minimal impact; over 20% is high impact.
4. **User adoption rate** = (active users ÷ target users) × 100. Above 80% in the first 90 days is strong; below 60% needs intervention.
5. **Data migration accuracy** = (clean records migrated ÷ records attempted) × 100. Above 98% before go-live; below 95% should delay cutover.

SPI and CPI come from earned value management. They only work if "earned value" is measured honestly: a task 90% done for three weeks is not 90% of its value.

## Who reviews what, and when

A KPI without a review rhythm is decoration. This is the cadence to set up.

<explainer-timeline title="When each set of KPIs gets reviewed" caption="Weekly in delivery, daily straight after go-live. A monthly review finds a slip once it is already structural." items="Delivery => Weekly programme board|Days 1-30 => Daily hypercare review|To day 90 => Weekly adoption review|Months 6 and 12 => Sponsor and CFO review" notes="Schedule, cost, risk, test pass rate, scope change. Gate KPIs go to the SteerCo|Availability, ticket volume, adoption by department|Adoption, process cycle times, ticket categories|Productivity, savings delivered, ROI" highlight="2"></explainer-timeline>

| When | KPIs | Reviewed by | Decision it feeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly during delivery | Schedule adherence, cost variance, risk resolution, test pass rate, scope change volume | Programme board | Re-plan, escalate or hold scope |
| At each phase gate | Configuration progress, training effectiveness, data migration accuracy, partner performance | Steering committee | Pass, conditional pass or stop |
| Daily in the first 30 days after go-live | Availability, ticket volume and trend, adoption by department | Hypercare lead | Where to send floor support and fixes |
| Weekly to day 90 | Adoption, process cycle times, ticket categories | Programme board | Refresher training, configuration fixes |
| At 6 and 12 months | Productivity, savings delivered, ROI, satisfaction | Sponsor and CFO | Business case sign-off, phase 2 scope |

One of my pharma clients assigned a specific owner, and a backup, to each milestone. Its schedule adherence improved dramatically compared with its previous SAP attempt. By the time a monthly review surfaces a slip, it is already structural.

Gate decisions should be evidence-based, not calendar-based. And by month three after go-live, workarounds have become habits, so the adoption window closes faster than most teams expect. ## KPIs for cloud programmes

RISE with SAP and SAP GROW programmes add governance questions the legacy list does not cover. Three extra measures help.

### Clean core level mix

SAP now grades extensions on [four clean core levels](https://news.sap.com/2025/08/extend-sap-s4hana-cloud-right-way-clean-clear/), A to D. Level A uses only released APIs; level D is not clean. Track the share of extensions at each level, using the ABAP test cockpit checks SAP recommends.

On the public edition, everything is level A by design. On the private edition and on-premise, any level C or D extension is debt that will surface at the next upgrade. Review new extension requests against this weekly during Realize, and make someone accountable for every level C or D approval.

### Extension placement decided

Formula: (extensions with an agreed level and location ÷ total extensions in the backlog) × 100. Target 100% by the end of Explore. An extension nobody has placed yet is the one that ends up as a classic modification under deadline pressure.

### SAP relationship health

A qualitative quarterly review on RISE programmes, where SAP runs infrastructure and operations and is part of delivery. Are platform escalations resolved within the agreed service levels? Are SAP's success reviews substantive or ceremonial? A weak score usually precedes a mid-programme escalation the team is not ready for.

## Where AI helps with KPI reporting

AI is useful for the reporting work around the metrics. It does not replace the review.

1. **Joule with SAP Cloud ALM.** [SAP has added Joule to Cloud ALM](https://support.sap.com/en/alm/sap-cloud-alm/operations/expert-portal/joule-agentic.html), so teams can query project and operations data in plain language rather than building every status extract by hand.
2. **Copilot in Power BI.** Drafts the narrative summary for a steering pack from the dashboard underneath. Works best when the data model is clean.
3. **Anomaly detection.** Power BI, Tableau and SAP Analytics Cloud can flag KPIs that drift from their usual pattern. Worth it on resource utilisation, ticket volume and scope change rate. Not worth it on metrics with high natural variance, such as daily order counts.

What AI does not solve is the political work. A dashboard can show schedule slippage in red for six weeks. If the steering committee doesn't act, the slippage continues.

## The adoption problem

The highest-impact KPI is user adoption, and it is the one most teams measure last.

I had a manufacturing client whose executives were exporting everything to Excel. Huge red flag. The data was there; the dashboards they needed weren't. We fixed the dashboards and cut decision time in half.

A system that works technically but gets bypassed in practice has delivered nothing. Change management research backs the point: Prosci's long-running studies find projects with excellent change management are [about seven times more likely to meet their objectives](https://www.prosci.com/blog/the-correlation-between-change-management-and-project-success) than those with poor change management.

The best KPI dashboard is not the most complete one. It is the smallest set the steering committee will look at, with an owner for each line and a consequence when red persists for two cycles. Most KPI programmes fail because the right things are tracked and then ignored. ## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is the most important ERP implementation KPI?</summary>

User adoption rate. A technically successful implementation nobody uses delivers no business value. The other KPIs (schedule, budget, testing) protect the conditions for adoption. Adoption tells you whether it happened.

Track it from week one after go-live, by department. Low adoption in one team usually points to a training gap or a process design problem you can still fix in hypercare.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How often should ERP implementation KPIs be reviewed?</summary>

Schedule, cost and risk weekly during delivery, not monthly at the steering committee. Phase-gate KPIs at each gate. Operational KPIs daily for the first 30 days after go-live, then weekly to day 90.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is a healthy testing pass rate for SAP UAT?</summary>

Above 85% first-time pass in system integration testing is healthy. Below that usually means process design gaps or configuration errors rather than isolated bugs.

If you go into UAT below 85%, stop and fix the root cause. UAT almost never cleans up what SIT missed.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What does a scope change percentage above 20% mean for an ERP project?</summary>

The project is being redesigned mid-flight. Overruns and delays become likely.

The trend matters more than the number. If changes accelerate as the project matures instead of settling, governance is failing. Every approved change needs a cost and schedule impact statement. If it doesn't have one, scope is out of control.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What KPIs are specific to RISE with SAP programmes?</summary>

Three on top of the standard 30: the clean core level mix of your extensions (A to D), the share of extensions with an agreed level and location, and a quarterly review of SAP relationship health (escalations, service levels, quality of SAP's success reviews).

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do you calculate ROI on an ERP implementation?</summary>

ROI = (net benefits ÷ total investment) × 100. Net benefits are measurable savings and revenue gains attributable to the system, minus the running cost of the new environment. Total investment covers software, implementation, internal time, training, data migration and ongoing support.

Be conservative. Full benefits rarely arrive in year one. Build a ramp-up model: 50% of steady-state benefits in year one, 80% in year two, 100% from year three.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the main causes of ERP implementation budget overruns?</summary>

Untracked scope changes, data migration that runs well past plan because quality problems surface late, integration failures found in testing, and change management that starts too late and drives up post-go-live support.

Weekly cost variance tracking and formal scope control handle the first. Early data quality assessment handles the second. Early integration testing with realistic volumes handles the third. Change management from the start handles the fourth.

</details>
