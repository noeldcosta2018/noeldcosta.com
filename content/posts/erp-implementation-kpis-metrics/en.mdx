ERP implementation KPIs are the difference between a programme that catches problems in week 8 and one that discovers them in month 18. Across 21 SAP programmes that I have led, rescued, or watched go sideways over 20+ years, the metrics that actually matter are not the longest list of dashboards. They are the small set of signals that get reviewed weekly during delivery and daily during hypercare, with owners named and consequences attached.

On a European manufacturing programme in 2024, the client added 73 "small" changes over the course of their SAP project. None of them individually looked significant. Collectively, they added five months to the timeline. Nobody had been tracking scope change volume.

On a European manufacturing programme in 2023, the client ignored early schedule warnings. Their one-year project took eighteen months. On a GCC retail programme in 2024, the client let consultant hour costs run 40% over without catching it. By the time they noticed, it was too late to course-correct.

These are not unusual failures. They are what happens when teams track the wrong things, or track nothing at all. Here are the 30 KPIs I use on ERP implementations, organised by phase, plus the three additional KPIs that the RISE era introduced and the AI tooling that accelerates the reporting workflow in 2026.

## During the project (KPIs 1 to 15)

| # | KPI | What it measures | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Schedule adherence** | Actual vs planned task completion | First signal of cascading delays |
| 2 | **Cost variance** | Actual spend vs budgeted spend by phase | Catches overruns before they compound |
| 3 | **Scope change volume** | Number and impact of approved changes | Uncontrolled changes are the most common cause of overruns |
| 4 | **Resource utilisation** | Hours worked vs planned; workload balance | Overloaded people quit or burn out mid-project |
| 5 | **User adoption rate** | Percentage of target users actively using the system | The only metric that tells you if the system is actually working |
| 6 | **Training effectiveness** | Assessment scores; percentage of users completing training | Predicts adoption failures before go-live |
| 7 | **Data migration accuracy** | Percentage of records migrated clean; error rate | Garbage data in a new system costs months to clean up |
| 8 | **System downtime during testing** | Hours of unplanned downtime in test environments | Instability in testing predicts instability at go-live |
| 9 | **Engagement score** | Survey results; attendance at key sessions | Early warning of political resistance before it surfaces publicly |
| 10 | **Risk resolution rate** | Percentage of open risks resolved on schedule | Unresolved risks compound; measure closure, not just identification |
| 11 | **Vendor performance** | Deliverable quality; milestone completion rate | Vendors that miss early deliverables almost always miss later ones |
| 12 | **Testing pass rate** | Percentage of test cases passing on first execution | Below 85% in SIT usually means systemic issues, not random bugs |
| 13 | **Change request turnaround** | Average days from request to decision | Long queues signal governance failure |
| 14 | **Budget utilisation rate** | Actual spend vs total budget as a percentage | Tracks burn rate against work completed |
| 15 | **Configuration progress** | Percentage of planned configuration items completed | Slippage here pushes testing and training downstream |

## Post-go-live (KPIs 16 to 30)

| # | KPI | What it measures | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | **System availability** | Percentage uptime after go-live | Over 99.9% optimal; under 99% is a user confidence problem |
| 17 | **Real-time data access** | Report load times; dashboard refresh rates | If managers export to Excel, the system is not delivering |
| 18 | **Employee productivity** | Task completion time vs pre-go-live baseline | On a European distribution programme in 2024, the client processed 25% more transactions per day post-go-live |
| 19 | **First contact resolution** | Percentage of support tickets resolved on first contact | Measures hypercare effectiveness |
| 20 | **Support ticket volume** | Open tickets; average resolution time | Volume spike at day 30 signals training gaps, not system bugs |
| 21 | **Process cycle times** | Order processing, invoice approval, close cycle | The measurable business outcome your executives actually care about |
| 22 | **Inventory accuracy** | Physical vs system counts | Most visible data quality indicator post-go-live |
| 23 | **Order fulfilment rate** | Percentage of orders fulfilled on time using new system | Direct operational impact measure |
| 24 | **Revenue growth attribution** | Revenue changes linked to new process capabilities | Long-term value proof for the business case |
| 25 | **Compliance adherence** | Audit pass rate; regulatory findings | Matters for finance, pharma, and regulated industries |
| 26 | **Demand forecasting accuracy** | Forecast vs actual demand | Tests whether planning modules are being used and trusted |
| 27 | **Employee satisfaction** | System usability survey; NPS from key users | If users hate the system, they will find workarounds |
| 28 | **Process efficiency improvement** | Time/cost per process vs pre-go-live | Justifies the investment to the board |
| 29 | **Cost savings delivered** | Actual savings vs business case projection | The CFO will ask for this at 6 months and 12 months |
| 30 | **Return on investment** | Net benefits divided by total implementation cost | Full ROI calculation; typically measured at 12 and 24 months |

## Five KPIs with formulas

These are the ones most frequently asked about.

### Schedule performance index (SPI)

Formula: Earned Value divided by Planned Value.

Benchmark: above 1.0 is ahead of schedule; 1.0 is on time; below 1.0 is delayed.

### Cost performance index (CPI)

Formula: Earned Value divided by Actual Cost.

Benchmark: above 1.0 is efficient; below 1.0 is over budget.

### Scope change percentage

Formula: (Total Approved Changes divided by Initial Scope Items) times 100.

Benchmark: under 10% minimal impact; over 20% high impact.

### User adoption rate

Formula: (Active Users divided by Total Target Users) times 100.

Benchmark: above 80% in the first 90 days is strong adoption; below 60% needs intervention.

### Data migration accuracy

Formula: (Clean Records Migrated divided by Total Records Attempted) times 100.

Benchmark: above 98% required before go-live; below 95% should delay cutover.

## What to track and when

### Weekly during delivery

Schedule adherence, cost variance, risk resolution rate, testing pass rate, scope change volume. These cannot wait for monthly steering committee reviews.

On a UK pharmaceutical programme in 2024, the team assigned specific owners to each milestone and reviewed weekly. They dramatically improved adherence compared to a previous SAP attempt that had tracked monthly. By the time a monthly review surfaces a slippage, it is already structural.

### At each phase gate

Configuration progress, training effectiveness, data migration accuracy, vendor performance. Gate decisions should be evidence-based, not calendar-based.

### First 90 days post-go-live

System availability, support ticket volume and trend, user adoption rate, process cycle times. The window to influence user behaviour closes faster than teams expect. By month three, workarounds become habits.

### At 6 and 12 months

Productivity measures, cost savings delivered, ROI, employee satisfaction. These are the numbers that validate the business case to the board.

## The three RISE-era KPIs

RISE with SAP and GROW with SAP introduced governance dynamics that the legacy KPI list does not cover. For programmes on SAP Cloud editions in 2026, three additional metrics matter.

### Clean-core compliance rate

Formula: (Customisations on SAP BTP under clean-core principles divided by Total customisations) times 100.

Benchmark: 100% on SAP Cloud editions because the platform enforces it. Below 100% on on-premise programmes is a signal of accumulating technical debt that will surface at the next major upgrade. Track customisation requests against this metric weekly during Realize.

### BTP extension coverage

Formula: (Business processes extended on SAP BTP divided by Total business processes requiring extension) times 100.

Benchmark: 100% by Deploy phase. Anything less means there are customisation requirements that have not been moved onto the strategic extension platform. The remaining customisations are technical debt that the upgrade cycle will expose.

### SAP customer success engagement score

Qualitative metric. On RISE programmes, SAP itself is a delivery participant with a customer success cadence. The engagement score tracks whether SAP is actively involved in the right way: platform escalations addressed within SLA, customer success reviews substantive rather than ceremonial, clean-core review forum decisions on track.

This is not a KPI for a dashboard. It is a quarterly governance review point. A low score correlates with mid-programme escalations into SAP that the project team is not prepared for.

## How AI changes KPI reporting in 2026

The 2024-2025 AI rollout reached metrics workflows. Three capabilities are useful now.

### Joule for SAP Activate

Generates first-draft KPI reports from SAP Cloud ALM and SAP Solution Manager source data. Saves 30 to 50% of effort on the weekly status reports that previously took the longest to compile. Still requires human validation, but the draft removes the manual extraction work.

### Microsoft Copilot for Power BI

Generates natural-language summaries of dashboard data. Useful for the executive-summary layer of steering committee packs where the underlying dashboard is detailed but the narrative is short. Works best when the underlying data is well-structured.

### Anomaly detection on dashboards

Modern AI overlays on Power BI, Tableau, and SAP Analytics Cloud flag KPIs that deviate from established patterns. Worth using on resource utilisation, support ticket volume, and scope change rate. Not worth using on metrics with high natural variance like daily order counts.

What AI does not solve: the political work of getting people to act on the metrics. The dashboard can show schedule slippage in red for six weeks; if the steering committee does not act on it, the slippage continues. The metrics are the signal. The action is human.

## The adoption problem

The highest-impact single KPI is user adoption rate, and it is the one most teams measure last.

On a GCC manufacturing programme in 2024, the client's executives were exporting every report to Excel within two weeks of go-live. The ERP data was accurate. The dashboards were configured. But nobody had built the reports the executives actually needed. We fixed the dashboards. Decision cycle time dropped by half.

A system that works technically but gets bypassed in practice has delivered zero value. Adoption tracks whether the system is working for the business, not just for IT.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is the most important ERP implementation KPI?</summary>

User adoption rate. A technically successful implementation that people do not use delivers zero business value. All the other KPIs (schedule, budget, testing) exist to protect the conditions under which adoption can happen. Adoption is the measure of whether they did.

Track it from the first week of go-live, broken down by department. Low adoption in specific teams usually reveals training gaps or process design problems that can still be fixed in hypercare.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How often should ERP implementation KPIs be reviewed?</summary>

Schedule, cost, and risk KPIs should be reviewed weekly during delivery, not monthly at steering committee. By the time a monthly review surfaces a problem, it is usually already structural.

Phase gate KPIs (configuration progress, data migration accuracy, testing pass rates) should be reviewed at each gate decision. Post-go-live operational KPIs should be tracked daily in the first 30 days, then weekly through month three.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is a healthy testing pass rate for SAP UAT?</summary>

A first-execution pass rate above 85% in system integration testing (SIT) is generally healthy. Below 85% usually signals systemic issues: process design gaps or configuration errors, rather than isolated bugs.

If test pass rates are below 85% going into UAT, the team needs to stop and resolve the root cause rather than proceeding on the assumption that UAT will clean up what SIT missed. It almost never does.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What does a scope change percentage above 20% mean for an ERP project?</summary>

Above 20% indicates high-impact scope changes relative to the original baseline. At this level, the project is essentially being redesigned mid-flight. Budget overruns and timeline extensions become highly likely.

The more meaningful signal than the number itself is the trend. If scope changes are accelerating rather than stabilising as the project matures, governance is failing. Every approved change should include an impact statement on cost and schedule; if it does not, the project manager has lost control of scope.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What KPIs are unique to RISE with SAP programmes?</summary>

Three additional KPIs matter on RISE and GROW with SAP programmes that do not apply to on-premise.

Clean-core compliance rate: percentage of customisations on SAP BTP under clean-core principles. Should be 100% on SAP Cloud editions because the platform enforces it.

BTP extension coverage: percentage of business processes requiring extension that are extended on SAP BTP rather than in-system. Should be 100% by Deploy phase.

SAP customer success engagement score: qualitative metric tracking whether SAP is actively engaged in the right way on RISE programmes (platform escalations addressed within SLA, customer success reviews substantive, clean-core review forum decisions on track).

These supplement the standard 30 KPIs; they do not replace them.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do you calculate ROI on an ERP implementation?</summary>

ROI equals (Net Benefits divided by Total Investment) times 100.

Net benefits are total measurable savings and revenue gains attributable to the system, minus ongoing operational costs of the new environment. Total investment covers software, implementation fees, internal resource time, training, data migration, and ongoing maintenance.

A realistic ROI calculation requires conservative benefit assumptions: full benefits rarely materialise in year one. Build a ramp-up model: 50% of steady-state benefits in year one, 80% in year two, 100% from year three.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the main causes of ERP implementation budget overruns?</summary>

The most consistent causes: untracked scope changes accumulating without impact assessment, data migration taking two to three times longer than planned due to quality problems discovered late, testing revealing integration failures that require significant rework, and change management starting too late, which drives post-go-live support costs up dramatically.

Tracking cost variance weekly and holding scope changes to a formal governance process addresses the first two. Early data quality assessments address the third. Change management embedded from the start, not added at training, addresses the fourth.

</details>

The best ERP implementation KPI dashboard in 2026 is not the most comprehensive one. It is the smallest set of metrics that the steering committee will actually look at, with owners named for each, and consequences attached when red persists for two cycles. Most KPI programmes fail not because the wrong things are tracked but because the right things are tracked and ignored. The discipline is in the review, not in the dashboard.
