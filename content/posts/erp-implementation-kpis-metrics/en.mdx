A client added 73 "small" changes over the course of their SAP project. None of them individually looked significant. Collectively, they added five months to the timeline. Nobody had been tracking scope change volume.

Another client ignored early schedule warnings. Their one-year project took eighteen months. A third let their consultant hour costs run 40% over without catching it; by the time they noticed, it was too late to course-correct.

These aren't unusual failures. They're what happens when teams track the wrong things, or track nothing at all.

Here are the 30 KPIs I use on ERP implementations, organized by phase.

## During the project (KPIs 1–15)

| # | KPI | What it measures | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Schedule adherence** | Actual vs. planned task completion | First signal of cascading delays |
| 2 | **Cost variance** | Actual spend vs. budgeted spend by phase | Catches overruns before they compound |
| 3 | **Scope change volume** | Number and impact of approved changes | Uncontrolled changes are the most common cause of overruns |
| 4 | **Resource utilization** | Hours worked vs. planned; workload balance | Overloaded people quit or burn out mid-project |
| 5 | **User adoption rate** | % of target users actively using the system | The only metric that tells you if the system is actually working |
| 6 | **Training effectiveness** | Assessment scores; % of users completing training | Predicts adoption failures before go-live |
| 7 | **Data migration accuracy** | % of records migrated clean; error rate | Garbage data in a new system costs months to clean up |
| 8 | **System downtime during testing** | Hours of unplanned downtime in test environments | Instability in testing predicts instability at go-live |
| 9 | **Stakeholder engagement score** | Satisfaction survey results; attendance at key sessions | Early warning of political resistance before it surfaces publicly |
| 10 | **Risk resolution rate** | % of open risks resolved on schedule | Unresolved risks compound; measure closure, not just identification |
| 11 | **Vendor performance** | Deliverable quality; milestone completion rate | Vendors that miss early deliverables almost always miss later ones |
| 12 | **Testing pass rate** | % of test cases passing on first execution | Below 85% in SIT usually means systemic issues, not random bugs |
| 13 | **Change request turnaround** | Average days from request to decision | Long queues signal governance failure |
| 14 | **Budget utilization rate** | Actual spend vs. total budget as a percentage | Tracks burn rate against work completed |
| 15 | **Configuration progress** | % of planned configuration items completed | Slippage here pushes testing and training downstream |

## Post-go-live (KPIs 16–30)

| # | KPI | What it measures | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | **System availability** | % uptime after go-live | > 99.9% optimal; < 99% is a user confidence problem |
| 17 | **Real-time data access** | Report load times; dashboard refresh rates | If managers export to Excel, the system isn't delivering |
| 18 | **Employee productivity** | Task completion time vs. pre-go-live baseline | A distribution client processed 25% more transactions/day post-go-live |
| 19 | **First contact resolution** | % of support tickets resolved on first contact | Measures hypercare effectiveness |
| 20 | **Support ticket volume** | Open tickets; average resolution time | Volume spike at day 30 signals training gaps, not system bugs |
| 21 | **Process cycle times** | Order processing, invoice approval, close cycle | The measurable business outcome your executives actually care about |
| 22 | **Inventory accuracy** | Physical vs. system counts | Most visible data quality indicator post-go-live |
| 23 | **Order fulfillment rate** | % of orders fulfilled on time using new system | Direct operational impact measure |
| 24 | **Revenue growth attribution** | Revenue changes linked to new process capabilities | Long-term value proof for the business case |
| 25 | **Compliance adherence** | Audit pass rate; regulatory findings | Matters for finance, pharma, and regulated industries |
| 26 | **Demand forecasting accuracy** | Forecast vs. actual demand | Tests whether planning modules are being used and trusted |
| 27 | **Employee satisfaction** | System usability survey; NPS from key users | If users hate the system, they'll find workarounds |
| 28 | **Process efficiency improvement** | Time/cost per process vs. pre-go-live | Justifies the investment to the board |
| 29 | **Cost savings delivered** | Actual savings vs. business case projection | The CFO will ask for this at 6 months and 12 months |
| 30 | **Return on investment** | Net benefits ÷ total implementation cost | Full ROI calculation; typically measured at 12 and 24 months |

## Five KPIs with formulas

These are the ones most frequently asked about.

**Schedule performance index (SPI)**
- Formula: Earned Value ÷ Planned Value
- Benchmark: > 1.0 = ahead of schedule; = 1.0 = on time; < 1.0 = delayed

**Cost performance index (CPI)**
- Formula: Earned Value ÷ Actual Cost
- Benchmark: > 1.0 = efficient; < 1.0 = over budget

**Scope change percentage**
- Formula: (Total Approved Changes ÷ Initial Scope Items) × 100
- Benchmark: < 10% minimal impact; > 20% high impact

**User adoption rate**
- Formula: (Active Users ÷ Total Target Users) × 100
- Benchmark: > 80% in first 90 days = strong adoption; < 60% = intervention needed

**Data migration accuracy**
- Formula: (Clean Records Migrated ÷ Total Records Attempted) × 100
- Benchmark: > 98% required before go-live; below 95% should delay cutover

## What to track and when

**Weekly during delivery:** schedule adherence, cost variance, risk resolution rate, testing pass rate, scope change volume. These can't wait for monthly steering committee reviews.

A pharma client who assigned specific owners to each milestone and reviewed weekly dramatically improved their adherence compared to their previous SAP attempt. Contrast that with a client who tracked everything monthly; by the time slippage was visible, it was already structural.

**At each phase gate:** configuration progress, training effectiveness, data migration accuracy, vendor performance. Gate decisions should be evidence-based, not calendar-based.

**First 90 days post-go-live:** system availability, support ticket volume and trend, user adoption rate, process cycle times. The window to influence user behavior closes faster than teams expect. By month three, workarounds become habits.

**At 6 and 12 months:** productivity measures, cost savings delivered, ROI, employee satisfaction. These are the numbers that validate the business case.

## The adoption problem

The highest-impact single KPI is user adoption rate, and it's the one most teams measure last.

I had a manufacturing client whose executives were exporting every report to Excel within two weeks of go-live. The ERP data was accurate. The dashboards were configured, but nobody had built the reports the executives actually needed. We fixed the dashboards. Decision cycle time dropped by half.

A system that works technically but gets bypassed in practice has delivered zero value. Adoption tracks whether the system is working for the business, not just for IT.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is the most important ERP implementation KPI?</summary>

User adoption rate. A technically successful implementation that people don't use delivers zero business value. All the other KPIs (schedule, budget, testing) exist to protect the conditions under which adoption can happen. Adoption is the measure of whether they did.

Track it from the first week of go-live, broken down by department. Low adoption in specific teams usually reveals training gaps or process design problems that can still be fixed in hypercare.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How often should ERP implementation KPIs be reviewed?</summary>

Schedule, cost, and risk KPIs should be reviewed weekly during delivery, not monthly at steering committee. By the time a monthly review surfaces a problem, it's usually already structural.

Phase gate KPIs (configuration progress, data migration accuracy, testing pass rates) should be reviewed at each gate decision. Post-go-live operational KPIs should be tracked daily in the first 30 days, then weekly through month three.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is a healthy testing pass rate for SAP UAT?</summary>

A first-execution pass rate above 85% in system integration testing (SIT) is generally healthy. Below 85% usually signals systemic issues: process design gaps or configuration errors, rather than isolated bugs.

If test pass rates are below 85% going into UAT, the team needs to stop and resolve the root cause rather than proceeding on the assumption that UAT will clean up what SIT missed. It almost never does.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What does a scope change percentage above 20% mean for an ERP project?</summary>

Above 20% indicates high-impact scope changes relative to the original baseline. At this level, the project is essentially being redesigned mid-flight. Budget overruns and timeline extensions become highly likely.

The more meaningful signal than the number itself is the trend. If scope changes are accelerating rather than stabilising as the project matures, governance is failing. Every approved change should include an impact statement on cost and schedule; if it doesn't, the PM has lost control of scope.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do you calculate ROI on an ERP implementation?</summary>

ROI = (Net Benefits ÷ Total Investment) × 100

Net benefits = total measurable savings and revenue gains attributable to the system, minus ongoing operational costs of the new environment. Total investment = software, implementation fees, internal resource time, training, data migration, and ongoing maintenance.

A realistic ROI calculation requires conservative benefit assumptions: full benefits rarely materialise in year one. Build a ramp-up model: 50% of steady-state benefits in year one, 80% in year two, 100% from year three.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What are the main causes of ERP implementation budget overruns?</summary>

The most consistent causes: untracked scope changes accumulating without impact assessment, data migration taking two to three times longer than planned due to quality problems discovered late, testing revealing integration failures that require significant rework, and change management starting too late: driving post-go-live support costs up dramatically.

Tracking cost variance weekly and holding scope changes to a formal governance process addresses the first two. Early data quality assessments address the third. Change management embedded from the start, not added at training, addresses the fourth.

</details>
