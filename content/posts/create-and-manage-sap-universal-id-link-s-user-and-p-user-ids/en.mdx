If you have more than one SAP account (a personal P-User plus S-Users from employers or clients), SAP Universal ID is the personal account that holds them together. It changed twice in 2026. From 20 January it stopped being a login: you now sign in with the S-User or P-User ID, or the email on that user, and that user's own password. From 13 July manual linking went away. You add email addresses in SAP Account Manager and every user with a matching email attaches automatically. If you have not set one up, register at account.sap.com with a personal email, then add your work addresses.

When I was new to SAP, I spent several weeks trying to figure out how Universal IDs, S-Users and P-Users work together. Before Universal ID, keeping track of all those accounts was a nightmare: password spreadsheets and constant reset emails when you forget which credentials go where.

## S-User, P-User and Universal ID compared

The table sets out who owns each ID and what it is for.

| ID type | Who owns it | Use it for | When you leave |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P-User** | You | SAP Community, trials, developer content, SAP Learning | Nothing changes. It is yours |
| **S-User** | Your employer or client, under their SAP customer number | Support cases, software downloads, licence keys, system data in SAP for Me | The administrator deletes it, or it reaches its expiry date |
| **Universal ID** | You | Holds your S-Users and P-Users in one list, plus learning certificates and SAP Community reputation | You keep it. Add your new employer's email |

My P-User has followed me for about 16 years through different jobs. I have had six different S-Users as I moved between companies. Universal ID is where all of them sit in one list.

Use a personal email for your Universal ID, not your work email. SAP's own FAQ recommends the same. If you leave the company and lose the work mailbox, a personal address is your way back in.

## What changed in 2026

SAP made two changes this year. Both affect how you log in and how accounts link. The official detail is on [SAP's Universal ID page](https://www.sap.com/account/universal-id.html).

1. **20 January 2026: Universal ID login switched off.** Universal ID is no longer an identity provider. You sign in with the S-User or P-User ID, or the email on that user, plus that user's password. Authentication runs through SAP Cloud Identity Services. Users with several linked identities were asked to set a password for each one between August 2025 and January 2026. If you skipped that step, use Forgot password on each user.
2. **13 July 2026: linking by email address.** The manual link and unlink options are gone. Every S-User or P-User whose email is registered in your Universal ID attaches to it automatically. Accounts you linked by hand before July stay linked.

For consultants who carry several client S-Users, the January change is a step back. One login used to cover everything. Now each S-User has its own password again, and switching users means logging out and back in. Keep a note in your password manager of which S-User belongs to which client and customer number.

## Setting up Universal ID and adding accounts

### Step 1: Create your SAP Universal ID

1. Go to [account.sap.com](https://account.sap.com/) and click **Register**.
2. Enter your name and a personal email address, then set a password.
3. Accept the terms of use. The marketing opt-in is optional.
4. Enter the verification code from your inbox. It stays valid for 15 minutes.
5. Sign in to SAP Account Manager. Your new P-User is listed on the **Accounts** tab.

The whole thing takes a few minutes. Have the email addresses of your other SAP users to hand so you can add them straight after.

### Step 2: Add the email addresses of your other accounts

1. Sign in to SAP Account Manager at [account.sap.com](https://account.sap.com/).
2. Open the **Accounts** tab and click **Add accounts**.
3. Enter the email address used by the S-User or P-User you want to bring in.
4. Enter the passcode SAP sends to that address.
5. Check the list. Every user with that email now appears under your Universal ID.
6. Repeat for each address, then set your primary email on the **Personal Info** tab.

One Universal ID holds up to 10 email addresses and up to 100 S-Users or P-Users. Each user can sit under only one Universal ID, and all of them must belong to one person. SAP Knowledge Base Article 2882908 covers adding an extra email or user.

### Signing in day to day

SAP recommends signing in with the user ID (S or P followed by digits) when several of your users share one email address. If those users also share a password, email sign-in returns an error because more than one user matches.

To switch users, log out and sign in with the other one. SAP says in-session switching will come later. In the account selector, sort by login date to put your most recent user at the top.

## Getting an S-User or P-User

### Provisioning an S-User

S-Users come from your company's user administrator, not from SAP. SAP creates only the first super administrator for a new customer. After that, the administrator requests S-Users in the [User Management app in SAP for Me](https://support.sap.com/content/s4m/help/users/admin.html). The new user receives an activation link that is valid for 10 days. Ask the administrator for the authorisations your role needs:

1. **Support cases**: create and follow incidents
2. **Software download**: installations, patches and add-ons
3. **Licence keys**: request keys for systems
4. **System data**: maintain installations and systems

An S-User is tied to one customer number and cannot be moved to another. S-Users also carry an expiry date. Close to the end you can request an extension in SAP for Me, and the request lands in your administrator's queue.

If you cannot find your SAP admin, start with IT. In smaller companies, check with Finance. They almost always have S-User access for licence reasons and will know who the admin is.

On RISE with SAP and GROW with SAP programmes the model is the same. The client provisions S-Users for its consultants under the client's customer number, and deletes them when the engagement ends. That is expected. It is their account, not yours.

### Registering a P-User

Register at [account.sap.com](https://account.sap.com/) with your own email. That gives you a P-User and a Universal ID in one step. P-Users cover SAP Community, free trials such as the one in my [SAP BTP Cockpit guide](/master-the-sap-btp-cockpit-simple-steps/), developer resources and SAP Learning. They do not give access to licensed support or software downloads. That is what S-Users are for.

## Common access errors and fixes

Most problems after the 2026 changes come down to email addresses and passwords.

| Problem | Most likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A user is missing from your Accounts tab | Its email differs from the addresses in your Universal ID | Add that email under Accounts, or ask your administrator to correct the email on the S-User |
| User already associated with another Universal ID | It sits under an older Universal ID you created | Raise a case with SAP support (SAP's KBAs use component `XX-SER-UID`) to release it |
| Email sign-in fails | Several users share that email and password | Sign in with the S-User or P-User ID instead |
| Old password rejected after January 2026 | Each user now has its own password | Use Forgot password on that specific user |
| Password locked | Too many wrong attempts | Wait, or reset the password. Happens to me at least once a year |
| S-User inactive or expired | Administrator deleted it, or it passed its expiry date | Ask that company's administrator to extend or replace it. If it was from a previous job, let it go |
| Signed in but cannot create cases or download | The S-User lacks that authorisation | Request it in SAP for Me. Your administrator approves it |

If you cannot sign in at all, SAP lists its Customer Interaction Center (CIC) as the contact for Universal ID help. Recovery involves identity checks, so start early if you need access for a deadline.

## Checklist for joining and leaving a client

Consultants hit these problems at the start and end of engagements. This is the order I would work through.

1. **Before day one:** confirm your Universal ID has a personal primary email.
2. **Day one:** ask the client's administrator for an S-User under their customer number, created with the email you will use there.
3. **Same day:** add that email in SAP Account Manager and check the new S-User appears.
4. **First week:** request only the authorisations your role needs. The administrator approves them in SAP for Me.
5. **During the engagement:** sign in with the S-User ID rather than the email if several of your users share an address.
6. **Before you leave:** if you earned certificates under that S-User, make sure your personal email is in your Universal ID. Moving certificates to a new S-User needs a support case.
7. **After you leave:** expect the S-User to be deleted. Your P-User and Universal ID stay.

If the client also gives you BTP access, the login failures there are a different problem. My [BTP Cockpit troubleshooting guide](/sap-btp-cockpit-issues/) covers the 401 errors.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between a P-User and an S-User in SAP?</summary>

A P-User is personal. You register it yourself, it belongs to you, and it follows you through job changes. It gives you access to SAP Community, free trials, developer content and SAP Learning.

An S-User belongs to your employer or client. Their user administrator creates it in SAP for Me under their customer number. It gives access to licensed services such as support cases, software downloads and licence keys. When you leave, they delete it.

SAP Universal ID keeps both types in one list, along with your certificates and community reputation.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can I still log in with my SAP Universal ID?</summary>

No. Since 20 January 2026, Universal ID is no longer an identity provider. You sign in with an S-User or P-User ID, or the email address on that user, and that user's password. Universal ID now works as a wallet that holds your users, certificates and SAP Community reputation. You still sign in to SAP Account Manager at account.sap.com to manage it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do I link an S-User to my SAP Universal ID after July 2026?</summary>

You add the S-User's email address instead of linking the user. In SAP Account Manager, open the Accounts tab, click Add accounts, enter the email and confirm the passcode SAP sends you. Every S-User and P-User with that email then appears under your Universal ID automatically. Users you linked by hand before 13 July 2026 stay linked.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What email address should I use for my SAP Universal ID?</summary>

Use a personal email as your primary address, and add your work addresses alongside it. If you leave a company and lose the work mailbox, the personal address keeps your Universal ID reachable. SAP recommends the same in its Universal ID FAQ.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What happens to my SAP Universal ID when I change jobs?</summary>

Your Universal ID stays with you. Your old employer's administrator withdraws or deletes the S-User. If your new employer creates an S-User with an email already in your Universal ID, it attaches automatically. If it uses a new email, add that address in SAP Account Manager.

Your P-User and Universal ID are unchanged throughout.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How many accounts can I link to SAP Universal ID?</summary>

Up to 10 email addresses and up to 100 S-Users or P-Users per Universal ID. All of them must belong to you. SAP treats anything beyond that as managing users on someone else's behalf, and shared accounts lead to suspension.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Does SAP Universal ID change what I can access in SAP systems?</summary>

No. Each S-User keeps its own authorisations. If your S-User does not have download rights, adding it to a Universal ID does not add them. Your company's user administrator grants authorisations in SAP for Me.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What should I do if I cannot log in to SAP?</summary>

First check which user you are signing in with. Since January 2026 each S-User and P-User has its own password, so use Forgot password on that specific user. If several users share your email, sign in with the user ID instead.

If you have lost access to the email on the account, contact SAP's Customer Interaction Center or raise a support case. SAP will verify your identity before restoring access.

</details>
