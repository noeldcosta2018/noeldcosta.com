## Where I help

I work with companies that spent time and money on new systems but still can't trust their numbers. Reports don't match, dashboards look fine but feel off, and every meeting starts with "the data isn't ready yet."

Most of the time, the software didn't fail. What went into it did. That's where I come in.

1. **Assess your systems.** See what's working, what isn't, and what's quietly costing you money.
2. **Select the right ERP or AI platform.** Choose one that fits your business, so your team isn't forced to fit the tool.
3. **Audit your data.** Find the gaps, bad inputs and missing links that keep your numbers from lining up.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What experience do you have with ERP implementation across different industries?</summary>

25 years with ERPs. I started in manufacturing, then moved into finance, retail, defence and public sector projects. SAP is where I spend most of my time, and Oracle, IFS and Microsoft Dynamics come up depending on what the client already has.

Manufacturing companies call me when production planning goes wrong. Finance clients want real ROI from their implementations. Retailers need to see inventory across every location.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do you implement ERP without disrupting ongoing business operations?</summary>

I start with the people who do the work every day and agree which processes must keep running. Then we roll out in phases, one area at a time, and run the old and new systems side by side during the switch.

Training starts well before go-live, and everyone knows what is changing and when. Something always goes a little sideways. Good planning keeps it small.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Which ERP implementation methodologies do you follow?</summary>

SAP Activate for SAP, Oracle Unified Method for Oracle, Sure Step for Microsoft Dynamics and IFS's own framework for IFS. The phases are similar: discover, prepare, explore, realise, deploy and run.

I spend most of my time in Explore, building working prototypes so people see exactly what they will get. I scale the approach to the company: small businesses don't need every step, and large enterprises usually want more approval gates.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What is the difference between you and other ERP service providers?</summary>

I work directly with your leadership team, I have no quotas or extra services to sell, and I start from business outcomes. I also limit my client load on purpose, so your project gets proper attention, and I stay involved until it works.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How do you help us choose the right ERP?</summary>

First, we work out the problems you really need to solve. Industry and company size then remove about half the options. I check the budget including implementation, technical and integration needs, user adoption and vendor stability.

I narrow it to two or three realistic options, then run proof-of-concept sessions where vendors demonstrate your actual workflows. The right choice usually becomes obvious there.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do you help with ERP licence and contract negotiations?</summary>

Yes, and it's one of the most valuable things I do. SAP, Oracle and Microsoft each license differently, and vendors do well when customers arrive unprepared.

I help you work out what you actually need before talking to vendors. Then we negotiate on fair pricing based on real usage, long-term maintenance costs, implementation penalties and your rights to your data.

</details>
