The main SAP modules are Financial Accounting (FI), Controlling (CO), Materials Management (MM), Sales and Distribution (SD), Production Planning (PP), Quality Management (QM), Plant Maintenance (PM), Human Capital Management (HCM), Extended Warehouse Management (EWM) and Project System (PS). Behind them sit the technical modules: Basis, ABAP, SAP Business Technology Platform (BTP) and SAP Integration Suite.

In SAP S/4HANA, SAP now groups these modules into lines of business such as Finance, Sourcing and Procurement, Manufacturing, Supply Chain and Sales. The old module codes have not gone away. Consultants, job ads and configuration menus still use them every day.

## Functional and technical SAP modules: the difference

SAP modules fall into two groups.

1. **Functional modules** map to a business process. Finance closes the books, procurement buys, sales sells, production makes. Business users log in to these every day.
2. **Technical modules** run the system underneath. They cover administration, custom development, integration and extensions. Users rarely see them, but every functional module depends on them.

People also talk about industry modules, such as SAP S/4HANA Utilities or Retail. These sit on top of the core modules rather than replacing them. More on those below.

I've seen teams struggle not because the system didn't work, but because they didn't really know what each module was responsible for, or how changes in one could ripple into others. You don't need to learn every feature. You need to know enough to choose well.

## SAP functional modules list

The table below lists the core functional modules, what each one does, and where it sits in SAP S/4HANA today. The line of business names come from SAP's own Feature Scope Description for S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608.

| Module | Full name | What it does | S/4HANA line of business |
|---|---|---|---|
| FI | Financial Accounting | General ledger, accounts payable and receivable, asset accounting, financial close and statutory reporting | Finance |
| CO | Controlling | Cost centres, internal orders, product costing and margin analysis for internal reporting | Finance |
| MM | Materials Management | Purchase requisitions, purchase orders, goods receipt, inventory and invoice verification | Sourcing and Procurement, Supply Chain |
| SD | Sales and Distribution | Quotations, sales orders, pricing, deliveries and billing (order to cash) | Sales |
| PP | Production Planning | Material requirements planning (MRP), bills of material, routings and production orders | Manufacturing |
| QM | Quality Management | Inspection planning, inspection lots, results recording and quality notifications | Manufacturing |
| PM | Plant Maintenance | Maintenance notifications, work orders and preventive maintenance plans for equipment | Asset Management |
| HR / HCM | Human Capital Management | Employee records, organisation management, time management and payroll | Human Resources |
| SuccessFactors | SAP SuccessFactors | SAP's cloud HR suite: core HR (Employee Central), recruiting, learning, performance | Human Resources (separate cloud product) |
| EWM | Extended Warehouse Management | Bin-level storage, inbound and outbound processing, picking and warehouse tasks | Supply Chain (Warehousing) |
| PS | Project System | Work breakdown structures, networks, project budgets and costs | R&D / Engineering |
| TRM | Treasury and Risk Management | Cash management, liquidity forecasts, financial instruments and hedging | Finance |

A few modules sit outside that core list but come up often. Customer Service (CS) handles service orders and contracts, now under the Service line of business.

Environment, Health and Safety (EHS) handles incidents and compliance, listed under Manufacturing. Transportation and delivery processing sit under Supply Chain.

### How the core modules connect

No functional module works alone. A sales order in SD checks stock in MM, triggers production in PP if stock is short, and posts revenue to FI when it is billed. CO then picks up the cost and margin.

That chain is why a design decision in one module ripples into others. Change a pricing condition in SD and you change what lands in FI. Skip a quality inspection type in QM and goods may sit blocked in MM.

### HR: SAP HCM or SuccessFactors

HR is the module where the packaging has changed most. On-premise customers can run SAP HCM for SAP S/4HANA, which SAP still teaches through its own learning courses on personnel administration, time management and payroll.

SAP's cloud direction for HR is SuccessFactors. In S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition, the Human Resources scope covers core HR and time recording, plus integration with an external HR system. SuccessFactors is SAP's own option for that external system.

## SAP technical modules

Technical modules don't show up in a process map, but they decide whether the system stays stable, fast and connected. The common ones:

| Module | What it does |
|---|---|
| Basis | System administration: installation, user and role management, transports between systems, patching and performance monitoring |
| ABAP | SAP's programming language for custom reports, forms, interfaces and enhancements inside the ERP |
| SAP BTP | SAP Business Technology Platform: builds extensions and apps outside the ERP core, plus data, AI and integration services |
| SAP Integration Suite | Connects SAP and non-SAP systems through APIs, events and integration flows. The successor to SAP PI/PO |
| SAP BW/4HANA | Data warehousing for reporting across SAP and non-SAP sources |
| SAP Analytics Cloud | Dashboards, planning and analytics in the cloud |

Two dates matter here. SAP Learning states that SAP NetWeaver 7.5, the platform under SAP Process Orchestration, has mainstream maintenance until the end of 2027, with optional extended maintenance until the end of 2030.

SAP positions SAP Integration Suite as the target. My guide to [SAP CPI and Integration Suite](/sap-cpi/) covers that move.

For reporting and planning on top of the modules, see my [SAP Analytics Cloud guide](/sap-analytics-cloud/).

I've seen projects stall not because Finance wasn't configured properly, but because someone underestimated the effort needed to manage transports or didn't plan for interface testing. It's the kind of thing you don't notice until it goes wrong. And then, it's all you notice.

## How SAP S/4HANA packages the modules

In SAP ECC, the module was the unit everyone planned around. In SAP S/4HANA, SAP organises its scope by line of business, with the old modules sitting inside them.

SAP's Feature Scope Description for S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition 2608 (document version dated 8 October 2026) lists these lines of business:

1. Asset Management
2. Finance
3. Human Resources
4. Manufacturing
5. Professional Services
6. R&D / Engineering
7. Sales
8. Service
9. Sourcing and Procurement
10. Supply Chain

The same document lists industry scope separately, including Public Sector, Banking, Insurance, Oil and Gas, and Retail.

Three practical changes come with that packaging:

1. **Finance and controlling share one journal.** In S/4HANA, FI and CO postings land in the universal journal, so the reconciliation work between them shrinks. That is why many people now say "FICO" as one area.
2. **Warehouse management moved.** Classic Warehouse Management (WM) gave way to EWM, which runs embedded in S/4HANA or as a separate system.
3. **The codes stayed.** Line of business names are how SAP sells and documents the product. FI, MM and SD are still how project teams talk.

### Why the ECC dates matter for module planning

SAP announced in February 2020 that mainstream maintenance for the core applications of SAP Business Suite 7, which includes SAP ECC, runs until the end of 2027, with optional extended maintenance until the end of 2030. SAP also committed to maintaining S/4HANA until the end of 2040.

If you run ECC today, your module choices are tied to that migration. My [ECC to S/4HANA migration guide](/ecc-to-s4hana-migration/) covers the paths, steps and deadlines.

## Industry modules

The core modules show up in every implementation. Industry modules handle the processes they can't cover: utilities billing, oil and gas joint venture accounting, retail store operations, public sector budgets and grants.

I remember a utilities project where the client assumed SD would handle their billing. It didn't. Not even close.

We had to pivot fast to IS-U, which, honestly, should've been part of the discussion from day one.

Industry modules still run on FI, MM and the rest. They change how those pieces behave for your sector. If your industry has one, put it in scope early, even if you are not sure how deep you will go.

## Which SAP modules to start with

Most businesses don't switch on the full suite at once. In my experience, most companies start with finance, HR and procurement.

That makes sense. Every company needs to close its books, pay its people and buy what it uses. Sales, production, quality and maintenance follow once that base is stable, depending on what the business actually does.

When you choose, look at four things:

1. **Your processes first.** Map what the business does before you pick modules. A manufacturer needs PP and QM. A services firm may need PS far more.
2. **Integration points.** Every module you add creates interfaces with the others and with outside systems. Budget the testing.
3. **Industry requirements.** Regulated billing, grants or joint ventures may need an industry solution, not a core module.
4. **Your team's capacity.** Each module needs a process owner on the business side. If nobody owns it, it drifts.

Not every module needs to go live on day one. Trying to do everything at once rarely works out well.
