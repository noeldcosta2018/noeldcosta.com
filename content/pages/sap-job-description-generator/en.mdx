## What goes into a good SAP JD

A description that attracts the right candidates and screens out the rest covers six things:

1. **Role title with module specificity.** "SAP Consultant" is noise. "SAP FICO Consultant with New GL and parallel ledger experience" is signal.
2. **Responsibilities tied to project phase.** Design, build, test, cutover, hypercare, and AMS are different jobs. State which ones the role owns.
3. **Modules and version.** S/4HANA 2023 is not ECC 6.0. Cloud is not on-premise. Name what is in scope.
4. **Certifications and qualifications.** Required vs preferred. SAP Certified Application Associate, TOGAF, PMP, accounting credentials where relevant.
5. **Experience level.** Number of full lifecycle implementations, years in the module, industry exposure.
6. **Compensation band.** Day rate for contract, base plus bonus for perm. Range, not a single point.

## What makes an SAP JD different

Generic JD templates miss the parts that decide whether a candidate can actually do the work. Five things matter on an SAP role and almost never appear in a generic posting:

**Module depth, not breadth.** A FICO consultant who has touched 12 modules at a surface level is not the same person as one who has done two full New GL migrations. The JD should ask for depth in the specific module, with examples of the sub-areas (AR, AP, AA, CO-PA, PCA).

**S/4HANA vs ECC experience.** These are different products in 2026. ECC experience is still useful, but a brownfield-to-S/4HANA migration needs people who have done the conversion, not just the legacy system. The JD should name the version explicitly.

**Regional context.** A consultant who has delivered in the GCC understands VAT registration logic, Arabic localisation, and Ramadan working hours. A consultant who has only worked in the US does not. If the role is in-region, the JD should say so.

**Day rate vs permanent.** Contract roles need different framing. Day rate range, IR35 status where applicable, contract length, remote vs onsite. Perm roles need base, bonus, equity, benefits. Mixing the two confuses both.

**Implementation phase you are hiring for.** Design needs senior architects. Build needs experienced configurators. Cutover needs people who have lived through a go-live weekend. Hypercare needs patience and AMS skills. The JD should describe the phase the role joins.
