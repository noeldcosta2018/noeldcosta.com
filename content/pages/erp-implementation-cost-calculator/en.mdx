The estimate combines fixed and variable components.

## What ERP systems really cost

Most companies end up spending between **one and five percent of annual revenue** on ERP. The exact landing depends on size, industry, and how messy the current state is.

1. **Small business (under 200 employees):** typically $150K to $750K. Basic modules, fewer users, limited customisation.
2. **Mid-market (200 to 1,000 employees):** $1M to $5M. More modules, more integration, real data migration scope.
3. **Large enterprise (1,000+):** $3M to $20M+. Multi-country, regulatory complexity, multi-language, deep customisation.
4. **Global conglomerate (10,000+):** $10M to $50M+. Multi-region rollouts, change management at scale.

Industry matters too. I have watched a logistics client spend an extra $500,000 just connecting their ERP to a fleet tracking system. A bank I worked with spent $800,000 on compliance customisations that were not in the original scope. A healthcare provider discovered 15 years of duplicate patient records that took months to clean up.

## Cost breakdown by category

Across most programmes I have run, the spend lands roughly like this.

| Component | Typical share | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Implementation services | 30–45% | Consulting, configuration, project management |
| Software licensing | 15–25% | Core modules, user licences, add-ons |
| Internal team time | 10–20% | IT, SMEs, business leads (often invisible in the vendor quote) |
| Data migration | 5–15% | Extraction, cleansing, conversion, validation |
| Customisation and development | 5–15% | ABAP, Fiori apps, integrations |
| Training and change management | 5–10% | End-user training, change agents, communications |
| Infrastructure and hosting | 5–10% | Cloud subscriptions, network, middleware |
| Contingency buffer | 5–10% | Scope changes, delays, unforeseen requirements |

Companies that staying close to standard SAP processes land at the lower end. Companies that customise everything land at the higher end. That is the single most controllable variable on most programmes.

<summary>How does customisation level affect the cost?</summary>

Significantly. <details>
<summary>Is cloud cheaper than on-premise?</summary>

Not necessarily over a five-to-seven year horizon, which is the relevant comparison window for an enterprise system. Cloud has lower upfront cost and no infrastructure overhead, but the subscription accrues. On-premise has higher upfront investment but no recurring licence fee after purchase, with annual maintenance typically at around 22% of licence cost.

For organisations that have existing infrastructure capability and plan to run the system for seven-plus years, on-premise often delivers lower total cost. For organisations prioritising flexibility, faster deployment, and lower IT overhead, cloud wins.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What about ongoing maintenance and support?</summary>

Plan for 5 to 10 percent of total project cost per year in ongoing maintenance, support, and incremental enhancements. The calculator projects this over the years you specify so the multi-year view is on the board paper from the start.

Companies that under-fund hypercare (the first 30 to 90 days after go-live) often pay the equivalent of three months of contractor day-rates fixing what should have been caught in testing. The right hypercare investment is usually 3 to 5 percent of total project cost, and it should be in the contract before signature.

</details>
