## The formula

The estimate combines fixed and variable components specific to SAP pricing.

**Programme cost = (licence or subscription × user count × deployment multiplier) + partner implementation services + data migration + customisation and ABAP development + training and change + BTP and infrastructure + post-go-live support + contingency**

For RISE and S/4HANA Cloud the licence component is a subscription, sized by Full Use Equivalent (FUE) users and the edition. For on-premise S/4HANA it is a perpetual licence with annual maintenance at around 22% of licence cost. Country and customisation multipliers adjust the final range. ## What SAP implementations really cost

Most SAP programmes end up spending between **two and six percent of annual revenue** over the life of the implementation. The landing point depends on size, industry complexity, regulatory load, and how far you plan to deviate from standard.

1. **Small business (under 200 employees):** typically $150K to $500K. Limited scope, standard modules like FI, CO, MM, often Business One or GROW with SAP.
2. **Mid-market (200 to 1,000 employees):** $500K to $2M. Broader module set, real integration scope, RISE or S/4HANA Cloud Public Edition is common.
3. **Large enterprise (1,000+):** $2M to $10M+. Multi-entity, multi-country, FI/CO with parallel ledgers, regulatory complexity, often S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition or on-premise.
4. **Global conglomerate (10,000+):** $10M to $50M+. Multi-region rollouts, group consolidation, change at scale.

Industry matters. I helped a manufacturing client reduce their licence costs by 32% by fixing the mix and cutting modules they did not need. Another client was about to sign for premium support they did not require: one contract adjustment saved them $220,000 a year. The savings sit in the contract before anyone touches a configuration screen.

## Cost breakdown by category

Across most SAP programmes I have run, the spend lands roughly like this.

| Category | Typical share | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Partner implementation services | 30–45% | Tier-1 SI consulting, configuration, project management, SAP Activate delivery |
| Software licensing or subscription | 15–25% | S/4HANA editions, BTP services, RISE bundles, add-ons (Ariba, SuccessFactors, Concur) |
| Internal team time | 10–20% | Process owners, FI/CO leads, basis admins, business SMEs (rarely in the vendor quote) |
| Data migration | 5–15% | Legacy extraction, cleansing, SAP Migration Cockpit, validation |
| Customisation and ABAP development | 5–15% | ABAP, CDS views, Fiori apps, BTP extensions, integrations via CPI |
| Training and change management | 5–10% | End-user training, super-users, communications, SAP Enable Now content |
| BTP and infrastructure | 5–10% | BTP services, hyperscaler hosting for private cloud, network, middleware |
| Post-go-live support and contingency | 10–15% | Hypercare, AMS, scope changes, unforeseen requirements |

Companies that stay close to standard S/4HANA processes land at the lower end. Companies that customise everything land at the higher end. Customisation is the single most controllable variable on most SAP programmes.

<summary>How does customisation level affect SAP cost?</summary>

Significantly. Companies that customise extensively typically spend 30 to 50 percent more on implementation, and that overhead does not stop at go-live. Custom ABAP and Z-code has to be maintained, retested at every S/4HANA release, and handed over to the next team. The calculator applies a multiplier for Low, Medium, and High customisation that reflects this pattern.

The cheapest customisation decision is usually not to make it. Standard S/4HANA processes have been refined over decades. The threshold question is whether the deviation gives you competitive advantage or covers a regulatory requirement. If the answer is "that's how we've always done it," reconsider.

<details>
<summary>What about ongoing SAP maintenance and support?</summary>

Plan for 15 to 20 percent of total implementation cost per year in ongoing maintenance, application management services (AMS), and incremental enhancements. The calculator projects this over the years you specify so the multi-year view is on the board paper from the start.

Companies that under-fund hypercare (the first 30 to 90 days after go-live) often pay the equivalent of three months of contractor day-rates fixing what should have been caught in testing. The right hypercare investment is usually 3 to 5 percent of total project cost, and it should be in the contract before signature.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is RISE with SAP cheaper than running S/4HANA on-premise?</summary>

Not necessarily over a five-to-seven year horizon, which is the relevant comparison window. RISE has lower upfront cost, predictable subscription, and no hyperscaler procurement to manage, but the subscription accrues every year. On-premise S/4HANA has higher upfront licence cost and infrastructure spend, but no subscription, with annual maintenance at around 22% of licence cost.

For organisations with existing basis capability and a 7+ year horizon, on-premise often comes out lower on TCO. For organisations that want SAP to manage the stack and prefer Opex over Capex, RISE wins. The calculator lets you model both.

</details>
