## Where I stand on AI in ERP

AI on broken data is worse than no AI. Get the foundation right first.
Clean masters. Reconciled ledgers. Documented processes. Then bring
in models. Most of what's currently sold as "AI transformation" is
model-first. That's backwards.

When I bring AI into an engagement, the order is: data quality and
governance first, then targeted agentic workflows on the highest-pain
process, then broader rollout. Most clients want to skip steps one
and two. The ones who let me run the order get to step three. The
ones who don't, don't.

## Public sector versus private sector

I work across both. They're different animals, honestly.

Private sector moves fast. CEO makes a decision Monday, the project
starts the following month. Government is the opposite. I once waited
six months for security clearance to look at an existing system.

Both have their upside. Private companies can change direction
quickly. Government programmes, once moving, usually have actual
budgets behind them. Nobody's asking you to deliver a transformation
on petty cash.

The two failure modes are different too:

1. **Private sector failures are usually pace failures.** Pushed too
   fast, skipped the close discovery, broke the cutover.
2. **Public sector failures are usually governance failures.** Too
   many committees, too many sign-offs, scope frozen in concrete
   before anyone validated it against operations.

The fix is the same in both cases. Get the right senior person in
the room early. Keep them there for the duration.
